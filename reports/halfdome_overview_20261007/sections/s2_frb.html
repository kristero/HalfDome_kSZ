2.1 The question and the models

Every density and pressure model in this section is painted onto the same HalfDome lightcone halo catalogue. That is 85,224,251 halos (catalog_total_rows in the DM-map provenance files), with M200c stored in M☉/h and converted with h = 0.68, complete above 7.327 × 1012 M☉ (DEFAULT_HALFDOME_MASS_FLOOR_MSUN, generate_halfdome_z1_dm_mass_windows.jl:118). A density model gives an electron column, and hence a DM, along any line of sight. A pressure model gives a Compton-y map, y = (σT/mec2) ∫ Pe dl, the line-of-sight integral of the electron pressure Pe (σT is the Thomson cross-section). Three tests are applied. (i) For rays to a fixed source redshift zs, does the halo-DM distribution match a hydrodynamical simulation in which the gas is measured rather than modelled? The reference is IllustrisTNG300-1 (TNG300), a 205 h−1 Mpc hydrodynamical box. (ii) Does the cross-correlation of y with FRB DM match the one existing measurement, Takahashi+25, made with 71 localised FRBs on the Planck MILCA y map (MILCA is one of Planck's component-separation methods) and 31 on the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) map? (iii) Does the y auto-power match the lightcone of FLAMINGO L2p8_m9, a hydrodynamical simulation in a 2.8 Gpc box? Test (iii) constrains the pressure side independently of FRBs.

A few definitions are used throughout. A gNFW (generalised Navarro–Frenk–White) profile is the shape x̄γ(1 + x̄α)−(β+γ)/α, with x̄ = x/xc and x = r/R200c. R200c is the radius enclosing 200 times the critical density ρcrit(z), and M200c is the mass inside it. The Lee22 fits are normalised by n200 = 200 ρcrit(z) fb / (XH mp) (density) and P200 = 200 G M200 ρcrit(z) fb / (2 R200) (pressure). Here fb = Ωb/Ωm is the cosmic baryon fraction and XH = 0.76 is the hydrogen mass fraction. XGPaint converts gas mass to free electrons with the mass per free electron of fully ionised hydrogen and helium, mper e = me + [2XH/(1+XH)] mH + [(1−XH)/(2(1+XH))] 4mH ≈ 1.137 mp. The function that does this is XGPaint's ne2d (the projected, i.e. two-dimensional, electron column ne of a halo, computed from its projected gas density rho_2d). For the pressure, Pth denotes the total thermal pressure of electrons and ions. For fully ionised gas with XH = 0.76 the electrons carry the fraction ne/(ne + nion) = 0.88/1.70 = 0.5176 of it, which is the Pe = 0.5176 Pth used by XGPaint. The density fit used here is MNRAS Table A2 (arXiv v1 Table 8), and the pressure fit is arXiv v1 Table 7 (MNRAS Table A1); both are the no-concentration fits (LEE2022_PROFILE_REFERENCE, lee2022_frb_dm_profile.jl:11; header of lee2022_tsz_pressure_profile.jl). 2.2 From a halo catalogue to a DM per ray

Rays and hits

The halo-DM PDFs are not read off a pixelised map. The generator frb_map_generation/generate_halfdome_z1_dm_mass_windows.jl is driven by run_halfdome_z1_mass_histograms_120k.pbs on idark and by lee22_tsz_corrected/run_pdf_zseries_local.sh locally. It draws 120,000 distinct HEALPix pixel centres at NSIDE 4096 with a MersenneTwister seeded with 42 (draw_random_frb_pixels, line 597) and puts every source at the same redshift zs. It then streams the catalogue in chunks of 106 rows and keeps halos with 0 ≤ zhalo ≤ zs. For each kept halo it finds the ray pixels inside the halo's aperture θmax, the angle subtended by X R200c, with X = 1 for every PDF here. For each ray inside the aperture, it evaluates the halo's DM at the exact angle θ between the halo centre and the ray, and adds it to the ray's sum for every mass window that contains the halo. A ray hits a window when at least one halo of that window contributes. DM adds linearly over halos:

The cached chord-mean column is multiplied by the exact chord factor (next subsection). The bitmask loop adds the same contribution to every window containing the halo, so all windows (twelve in the main runs, thirteen in the 1014–1015 M☉ runs, at most sixteen with a UInt16 mask) come from one catalogue pass. What one halo contributes: gas inside the R200c sphere

A ray at impact parameter b through a halo at redshift z picks up the observer-frame column DM = (1+z)−1 ∫ ne dl, with ne and the path length in physical units. The factor 1/(1+z) converts the rest-frame column to the observed dispersion. TNG's "within R200" catalogue counts only gas cells inside each halo's R200 sphere. HalfDome therefore integrates only the chord inside the sphere, with x = b/R200c ≈ θ/θ200c:

DMh(x) = [2 R200c/(1+z)] ∫0L ne(√(x2 + l2)) dl, L = √(X2 − x2) for x < X, and 0 otherwise

The older convention was XGPaint's projected profile: the full line of sight to 105 R200c, cut only on the sky. Under it, every ray inside the aperture received the whole column at its impact parameter, and every ray just outside received nothing. That gave the PDF a hard floor and a missing low-DM tail (Figure 2.1).

Not an XGPaint edit: the R200c sphere replaces XGPaint's line of sight to 105 R200c

XGPaint's projected profiles integrate the 3-D profile along the whole ray: _nfw_profile_los_quadrature(x, xc, α, β, γ; zmax=1e5, ...) at src/profiles_y.jl:100 of the fork, which upstream WebSky-CITA/XGPaint.jl also has. Painting is cut only on the sky. The fix lives in project code. SphericalChordDMProfile and spherical_chord_factor are in frb_map_generation/lee2022_frb_dm_profile.jl:710-745, and the generator flag is --halo-boundary=spherical. The 3-D density still comes from XGPaint's get_params and generalized_nfw. frb_map_generation/WHAT_CHANGED_FOR_TNG_AGREEMENT_20260917.md §2 states that no line of XGPaint was modified for this. Effect on the numbers (same report): the low-DM floor disappears (first populated bin 0.2 instead of 25 pc cm−3), and the mean DM per hit falls by a factor 0.70–0.73 for both profiles. The report attributes this to the gas outside the sphere: about 27 % of the gas that the projected profile places inside a 1 R200c aperture lies outside the R200c sphere (the report words this as "27% of the projected column at 1 R200c"; at exactly b = R200c the whole projected column lies outside the sphere). A direct integration of the Battaglia16 profile done for this report gives 26–30 % for halos of 7 × 1012–5 × 1013 M☉ at z = 0.1–1, consistent with the 0.70–0.73. The y maps of Section 2.8 keep XGPaint's projected profile and a 4 R200c aperture.

spherical_chord_half_length(x::Real, sphere::Real) = x < sphere ? sqrt(sphere^2 - x^2) : 0.0

"""Exact chord factor used by the generator: 2 L_max at angular radius theta, given the
aperture edge theta_max = angular_size(X R200c)."""
@inline function spherical_chord_factor(theta::Real, theta_max::Real, sphere::Real)
    ratio = Float64(theta) / Float64(theta_max)
    ratio >= 1 && return 0.0
    return 2 * Float64(sphere) * sqrt(1 - ratio^2)
end

function (model::SphericalChordDMProfile{T})(theta_rad, mass_msun, redshift) where {T}
    theta = Float64(theta_rad); mass = Float64(mass_msun); z = Float64(redshift)
    theta > 0 || error("Angular radius must be positive.")
    r200c = getfield(XGPaint, Symbol("R_", Char(0x0394)))(model, mass * XGPaint.M_sun, z, 200)
    x = theta / XGPaint.angular_size(model, r200c, z)
    sphere = model.sphere_r200c
    lmax = spherical_chord_half_length(x, sphere)
    if lmax < 1.0e-6
        # At or beyond the edge: the chord-mean tends to the edge density; keep the
        # cached function continuous and positive (the generator never uses g there).
        r200c_m = Float64(ustrip(uconvert(u"m", r200c)))
        return T(halo_electron_density_m3(model.inner, sphere, mass, z) * r200c_m * M2_TO_PC_CM3_LOCAL / (1 + z))
    end
    return T(chord_dm_pc_cm3(model.inner, x, lmax, mass, z) / (2 * lmax))
end

The quantity cached on the (θ, M, z) interpolation grid is the chord mean g = DM/(2L), not DM. Unlike DM, g stays smooth and positive at the sphere edge, where it tends to the edge density, so it can be log-interpolated safely. The generator then multiplies by the exact chord factor 2X√(1 − (θ/θmax)2) for each ray, so DM goes to 0 continuously for a grazing ray. Each cache is checked against direct quadrature. For the corrected Lee22 cache the maximum relative error is 3.2 × 10−4, and the profile-owned chord check is 3.9 × 10−4 (cache.cache_check_max_relative_error and cache.profile_owned_chord_check_max_relative_error in frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_corrected_20260930/fullsky/maps/lee22_noconc_phys_sphere1_planck_provenance.txt).

The electron densities

For Battaglia16 the parameters come from XGPaint's get_params, for example P0 = 4000 (M/1014 M☉)0.29 (1+z)−0.66 with xc = 0.5 and γ = −0.2. The project builds the profile as XGPaint.HaloDMProfile(XGPaint.BattagliaTauProfile(Omega_c=0.261, Omega_b=0.049, h=0.68)) (frb_map_generation/lee2022_frb_dm_profile.jl:903), so the parameters are the BattagliaTauProfile defaults (XGPaint src/profiles_tau.jl:28-33), which are identical to the HaloDMProfile keyword defaults (src/profiles_frb.jl:16-21). The gas density is ρgas = P0 gNFW(x) fb ρcrit(z), and the free-electron density is 0.9 ρgas/mper e. For Lee22 the electron density is ne = N · s(z) · n0 f(x) n200, where f is the Lee22 gNFW shape, N is a normalisation reading and s(z) is a redshift factor. The two Lee22 densities used here differ only in N and s(z).

  • Lee22 XGPaint pipeline (previous production). The Lee22 parameters were handed to XGPaint as if n0 f(x) were a gas density in units of 200 fb ρcrit, the way XGPaint reads Battaglia16 (P0 = 200 n0). XGPaint's ne2d then supplied the electrons with its 0.9 and mper e. Relative to eq. 9 this is N = 0.9 XH mp/mper e = 0.6016, with s = 1.
  • Lee22 corrected (final). N = 1 (eq. 9 literally) and s(z) = [0.6774 (1+z)]3 XH2. The (1+z)3 undoes the comoving-volume bookkeeping of Lee22's extraction code. The hTNG3 = 0.67743 and XH2 undo the code units and hydrogen convention inferred for the unpublished n200 step (Section 1).

The ratio pipeline / corrected is 0.6016 / ([0.6774 (1+z)]3 XH2) = 3.35, 0.42 and 0.12 at z = 0, 1 and 2, independent of mass and radius (frb_map_generation/lee22_tsz_corrected/doc/lee22_calibration.html, version 4 of 2026-10-01, Fig. 2e; this document is cited below as the "Lee22 doc"). So the old density was 3.35 times too dense at z = 0, about right near z ≈ 0.5, and too dilute at high redshift. Most of the PDF results below follow from this.

function ne2d(model::AbstractBattagliaTauProfile, r, m200c, z)
    me = constants.ElectronMass
    mH = constants.ProtonMass
    mHe = 4mH
    xH = 0.76
    nH_ne = 2xH / (xH + 1)
    nHe_ne = (1 - xH)/(2 * (1 + xH))
    factor = (me + nH_ne*mH + nHe_ne*mHe)
    result = rho_2d(model, r, m200c, z)  # (Msun) / (Mpc)^2
    return 0.9 * result / factor
end

From DM values to PDFs, and the summary statistic

For each window the generator histograms the 120,000 ray DMs on 300 logarithmic edges between 0.1 and 30,000 pc cm−3 (PDF_EDGE_COUNT, PDF_DM_MIN and PDF_DM_MAX in the PBS script; histogram_columns, line 1351). Rays with DM = 0 fall below the first edge, so the stored quantity is the conditional PDF

p(DM | DM > 0)k = nk / (Nhit ΔDMk), ⟨DM⟩hit = Σk nk √(ek ek+1) / Σk nk

Here nk is the number of rays in bin k, with edges ek and ek+1. The hit fraction Nhit/Nrays is reported separately. In the figures, bins with fewer than 5 rays are not drawn. The residual Δ = 100 (pmodel/pTNG − 1) is shown only where both samples have at least 10 rays (frb_map_generation/lee22_tsz_corrected/pub/plot_pub_figures.py:67-100). The headline statistic is the mean DM per hit, ⟨DM⟩hit, rather than the mean over all rays. HalfDome's halos come from a gravity-only simulation, while TNG300's are hydrodynamical halos, and in the same M200c decade TNG300 has about 0.8 times as many (0.80–0.84, Lee22 doc §5). That number comes from Poisson-inverting the hit fractions: if halos are crossed at random, the mean number of crossings per ray is N̄ = −ln(1 − fhit), and the ratio of N̄ between the two catalogues is the ratio of halo abundances. HalfDome rays therefore hit more often: 8.1 % against 6.6 % at zs = 0.1, and 64 % against 58 % at zs = 3. The mean per hit removes most of this abundance difference and compares, to first order, the gas per halo crossing, which is the quantity the density model controls.

The mean per hit is not exactly the gas per crossing. A hit ray can cross several halos of the window. For Poisson crossings the mean over all rays is N̄ μc, with μc the mean DM per crossing, so ⟨DM⟩hit = μc N̄/(1 − e−N̄). With the hit fractions of the table in Section 2.4, N̄/(1 − e−N̄) is 1.04 for HalfDome against 1.03 for TNG at zs = 0.1, 1.44 against 1.34 at zs = 1 and 1.60 against 1.49 at zs = 3. HalfDome's higher abundance alone therefore raises its mean per hit relative to TNG by about 1 % at zs = 0.1, 4 % at zs = 0.5 and 7 % at zs ≥ 1. This is an estimate made for this report from the tabulated hit fractions, with clustering ignored; it is not in the source reports. The analytic model shows a sensitivity of the same order: in the window read as [1013, 1015), switching from TNG300's abundance to a plain Tinker08 abundance moves the corrected density's per-hit ratio from 1.02 / 1.01 / 0.99 to within 0.98–1.04, and with TNG300's own abundance the mean over all rays is 0.96–0.97 of TNG (Lee22 doc §5). Dividing the per-hit ratios of Section 2.4 by these factors, the corrected density carries about 0.94–0.97 of TNG's gas per halo crossing (0.97 at zs = 0.1, 0.94–0.96 at zs ≥ 0.5). The headline per-hit numbers are correct as defined, but they flatter the density by a few per cent.

Two related scripts. frb_map_generation/frb_fixed_redshift_dm_pdfs.jl is the original fixed-redshift generator; it used XGPaint's projected HaloDMProfile directly and is superseded. frb_map_generation/sample_halfdome_updated_sightlines.jl defines the DM model specifications and the 100,000 observed-redshift sightlines used to validate the cross-correlation maps (Section 2.6). Its uncommitted working-tree change only adds a lee22_noconc_phys_sphere3 spec and rewords one self-test message; no result in this section uses it.

2.3 The TNG300 reference and its mass windows

The reference is the pre-release TNG300-1 halo-DM catalogue of R. Konietzka. It has 120,000 rays for each source redshift zs = 0.1–5.5 in steps of 0.1 (arrays of shape 55 × 120,000). The DM is summed over gas cells within R200 of each halo, with star-forming gas given ne = 0 (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_analytic_tng_repro_20260930/README.md §1). Per-ray arrays DMhalo_r200_massAtoB are given for several mass ranges "A to B". The Konietzka et al. (2025) paper does not describe these windows (analytic-repro README §1), and how they are read decides the comparison.

Finding: each "A to B" array spans [10A, 10B+1) M☉, i.e. decades A through B inclusive. The evidence has two parts.

  1. An exact identity. On every ray, DM(10to14) = DM(10to12) + DM(13to14) holds to round-off. Under the nominal reading [10A, 10B), the right-hand side would leave out the 1012–1013 decade, so the identity could not hold. It holds when "10to12" means [1010, 1013) and "13to14" means [1013, 1015). Re-run for this report from the raw arrays (read-only), the maximum |DM(10to14) − DM(10to12) − DM(13to14)| over 120,000 rays is 1.1 × 10−13, 2.3 × 10−13, 4.6 × 10−13, 9.1 × 10−13, 4.6 × 10−13 and 4.6 × 10−13 pc cm−3 at zs = 0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 and 3. The plotting code asserts the same thing (< 10−10) for every panel of the zs-series figures (identity_residual, defined in tng_windows.py:31 and called at plot_pub_figures.py:115).
  2. Hit fractions. The difference DM(10to14) − DM(10to13), which is the 1014–1015 decade under the inclusive reading, is non-zero on 2.01 %, 8.31 % and 11.73 % of rays at zs = 0.1, 0.5 and 1 (same re-run). That is the rate expected from the 278 / 152 / 49 clusters in TNG300's z = 0 / 0.5 / 1 snapshots (2.0 / 8.3 / 11.6 %; analytic-repro README §4) and the rate HalfDome sees (2.0 / 8.7 / 11.9 %; Lee22 doc §3). The nominal 1014–1015 difference, DM(10to15) − DM(10to14), is non-zero on only 0.065 %, 0.231 % and 0.263 % of rays: it is the 1015–1016 decade.

def tng_exact(direct, label, zs):
    """Per-ray DM [pc cm^-3] of TNG halos in the true mass range named by `label` (HalfDome window labels, [lo, hi))."""
    if label == "m1e12_to_1e14":     # [1e12,1e15) minus the 1e14-1e15 decade
        v = np.asarray(direct.tng_values("m1e12_to_1e14", zs), float) - tng_exact(direct, "m1e14_to_1e15", zs)

    plus, minus, _ = EXACT[label]
    v = np.asarray(direct.tng_values(plus, zs), float)
    if minus is not None:
        v = v - np.asarray(direct.tng_values(minus, zs), float)
    return np.where(v > 1e-9, v, 0.0)  # differences of identical sums carry ~1e-13 round-off

Every TNG curve in this section is built here. DM adds over halos, so a single decade follows ray by ray from differences of nested arrays. The true 1013–1014 decade is DM(10to13) − DM(10to12). The 10−9 threshold removes the ~10−13 round-off left when identical sums are subtracted, so rays that contain no halo of the decade stay at exactly zero and are not counted as hits.

Consequences. The array labelled "13to14" holds halos of 1013–1015 M☉. Its mean DM per hit is 1.42, 1.41, 1.36, 1.32, 1.30 and 1.29 times that of the true decade at zs = 0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 and 3 (column mean_over_tng in frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_pdf_zseries_20261001/analysis/halo_pdf_zseries_stats.csv). The empirical C(z) = 0.284 (1 + min(z, 2))2.48 (redshift_scaling = :tng_calibrated) was fitted to it and is 1.3–1.5 times too high against the true decade. The intermediate "corrected Lee22 is 22–31 % low" statements were also made against it. Both are superseded. Figure 2.2 shows the investigation that led to the reading. No alternative boundary or electron convention reproduces both the extra electrons and the unchanged hit fraction; counting the clusters does.

The window reading follows from the arrays alone: the identity holds on every ray. R. Konietzka has not yet confirmed the labelling convention ("decades A..B inclusive"). This is the first open item in Section 2.10.

2.5 Cross-check with an analytic halo model

The rays are checked against an independent calculation: the clustered compound-Poisson halo model of Torkamani et al. (2026), evaluated with exactly the same densities. In a thin redshift slice, the number of halos a ray crosses is Poisson distributed with mean N̄. Each crossing adds a DM drawn from the single-halo distribution set by the halo mass function, the profile and the impact-parameter area. The characteristic function (Fourier transform) of the total is then exp[N̄ (φ(λ) − 1)], where φ is the single-crossing characteristic function. Halo clustering adds a Gaussian term in the bias-weighted crossings, scaled by the projected matter variance.

        return 0.5 * amp * A * A, 0.5 * amp * g["nbar_b"] ** 2

        mean_all += s["mean_dm"]

        group["dep_b"] += dd_b

        group["z_w"] += s["z"] * s["dz"]; group["dz"] += s["dz"]
        if verbose:

Here total accumulates, on a DM lattice, the expected number of crossings per DM cell (area weights times abundance), summed over redshift slices. np.fft.rfft(total) − nbar is the log characteristic function N̄(φ − 1) of the unclustered compound-Poisson sum. flush adds the clustering term ½ A(λ)2, where A is the transform of the bias-weighted deposit (Tinker10 bias) minus its value at zero frequency, the bias-weighted N̄. Its amplitude is H(z)/c · D(z)2 · S/Δz, where D is the linear growth factor, S the projected matter variance (cosmo.projected_variance()) and Δz = 0.05 the width of a group of slices (clustering_dz). Inverting exp(log_u + log_cl) gives the clustered PDF on the lattice; the DM = 0 spike, exp(−N̄) times the clustering correction, is stored separately as the zero fraction.

Run with the HalfDome catalogue's own halo abundance, the analytic model reproduces the rays' mean DM per hit to ≤ 1 % at zs = 0.5 and 1. At zs = 0.1 it agrees to 2–3 % for the Lee22 densities, and is 8 % low for Battaglia16 (Lee22 doc §5; halo_pdf_corrected_lee22_stats.csv lists 78.8 rays against 72.4 analytic for Battaglia16 at zs = 0.1). Rays and model are thus interchangeable for these densities. The analytic model can also be run in TNG's own cosmology, which removes one possible confound. The cosmology difference is not what drives the agreement: the corrected density gives 0.95, 1.01 and 1.03 of TNG there (Figure 2.5). Figure 2.6 adds the superseded C(z) calibration and the 1012–1014 M☉ window, where the catalogue floor dominates.

Figure 2.7 supports three conclusions. First, Torkamani's published test is reproduced. It concerns the total DM, and BFC puts 83 % of its halo DM outside R200c, so it says little about halo interiors. Second, the window-free "all halos" array cannot be matched by any smooth profile in the HalfDome mass range, because most of TNG's halo DM sits in halos below HalfDome's mass floor. Third, the empty nominal 1014–1015 window is not a finite-box effect. Over 20,000 box realisations, the 99.9 % range of the expected hit fraction is 1.1–2.9 / 4.7–12.2 / 6.4–17 % at zs = 0.1 / 0.5 / 1, while the array difference is hit on only 0.065 / 0.23 / 0.26 % of rays (Lee22 doc §5). This independently confirms the window reading.

2.6 The tSZ × FRB DM estimator

The measurement

Takahashi et al. (2025, arXiv:2511.02155) measured wyDM(θ), the DM-weighted mean Compton-y in annuli around localised FRBs, with the same quantity around random positions subtracted. The authors supplied the binned measurement and its covariance. For each survey there are three files: the FRB term (a), the random term (b), and the leave-one-FRB-out jackknife covariance, all in 13 logarithmic bins of 0.25 dex. The measurement is (a) − (b). The processing script checks that √diag(C) equals the quoted σ and that bins 1–12 coincide with the repository's 1–1000′ grid to 10−6. Bin 0 (θ ≈ 0.75′, outside that grid) is kept in the loaded arrays but lies below both surveys' angular cuts, so it never enters χ² (TAKAHASHI25_REAL_OBSERVATIONS_20260919.md §2–3). Planck uses the MILCA y map (71 FRBs) and ACT uses 31 FRBs. The raw data folder Takahashi25_data/ is not committed, because its redistribution terms were not stated.

The model: an observed-redshift-weighted full-sky DM map

A model wyDM could be built from 71 mock FRBs, but it would carry the huge sample variance of 71 sightlines. Instead, every pixel of a full-sky halo-DM map is treated as a source, with each halo weighted by the fraction of the observed FRB redshift distribution that lies behind it:

DM(n̂) = Σh w(zh) DMh(n̂), w(zh) = Σi ωi I(zs,i ≥ zh) / Σi ωi

Here ωi = 1 is the weight of observed FRB i and I is the indicator function. The sky average ⟨y DM⟩ of this map equals the ensemble mean of the stratified sightline estimator, which groups mock sources by observed redshift and subtracts each group's mean DM and the random-position y mean (TSZ_DM_FULLSKY_20260918.md §4). That ensemble mean is the expected cross-signal for sources at uniformly random positions with exactly the observed redshift distribution. It is not a realisation of 71 or 31 FRBs.

function source_kernel(path)
    lines = readlines(path)
    first(lines) == "frb,redshift,weight" || error("Unexpected kernel CSV header in $(path)")
    rows = [split(line, ',') for line in lines[2:end] if !isempty(strip(line))]
    zs = [parse(Float64, row[2]) for row in rows]
    weights = [parse(Float64, row[3]) for row in rows]
    !isempty(zs) && all(isfinite, zs) && all(zs .> 0) || error("Invalid source redshifts")
    all(isfinite, weights) && all(weights .> 0) || error("Invalid source weights")
    order = sortperm(zs)
    zs, weights = zs[order], weights[order]
    tail = reverse(cumsum(reverse(weights))) ./ sum(weights)
    return SourceKernel(zs, vcat(tail, 0.0))
end

# A halo contributes only to sources behind it: fraction of the source weight with z_source >= z_halo.
source_fraction(kernel::SourceKernel, z) = kernel.tail_weight[searchsortedfirst(kernel.redshift, z)]

The self-test that follows in the file (test_source_kernel) checks that summing weighted halos equals averaging the individual source screens to 10−14. Each run of the painter fills all of its density models for both kernels in one catalogue pass. The nine 1-R200c-sphere DM maps used in the χ² grid below come from three such runs (2026-09-18, 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30) and are listed in corrected_spectra.py:23-33.

Spectra and the angular correlation

Both maps are mean-subtracted and transformed with healpy.map2alm (ℓmax = 8192, 3 Jacobi iterations, no pixel-window deconvolution). The cross-spectrum monopole is set to zero, and every cross-spectrum is checked against the Cauchy–Schwarz bound (CℓyDM)2 ≤ Cℓyy CℓDM DM (frb_map_generation/lee22_tsz_corrected/corrected_spectra.py:113-123). The survey beam is applied as a Gaussian to y only, because FRB positions are not smoothed: FWHM 10′ for Planck and 1.6′ for ACT. The correlation function and its exact annulus averages are then

w(θ) = Σℓ≥1 (2ℓ+1)/(4π) CℓyDM Bℓ Pℓ(cos θ), Bℓ = exp[−ℓ(ℓ+1)σb2/2], w̄[θ1,θ2] = ∫ w dμ / (μ1 − μ2), μ = cos θ
def gaussian_beam(ell, fwhm_arcmin):
    sigma = np.deg2rad(fwhm_arcmin/60.) / np.sqrt(8*np.log(2.))
    return np.exp(-0.5*ell*(ell+1)*sigma*sigma)


def angular_correlation(cl, theta_arcmin, beam_arcmin):
    ell = np.arange(len(cl))
    coefficients = (2*ell+1)/(4*np.pi)*cl*gaussian_beam(ell, beam_arcmin)
    coefficients[0] = 0
    return np.polynomial.legendre.legval(np.cos(np.deg2rad(np.asarray(theta_arcmin)/60.)), coefficients)


def annular_correlation(cl, lower_arcmin, upper_arcmin, beam_arcmin):
    """Exact solid-angle average: integral P_l(mu) dmu / (mu_hi-mu_lo).

    Legendre antiderivatives avoid using plotted/jittered points as bin centres.
    Only y is beam-smoothed; FRB positions are not smeared by a second beam.
    """
    lower = np.asarray(lower_arcmin)
    upper = np.asarray(upper_arcmin)
    if np.any(lower <= 0) or np.any(upper <= lower):
        raise ValueError("Invalid angular bins")
    ell = np.arange(len(cl))
    coefficients = (2*ell+1)/(4*np.pi)*cl*gaussian_beam(ell, beam_arcmin)
    coefficients[0] = 0
    primitive = np.polynomial.legendre.legint(coefficients)
    a, b = np.deg2rad(lower/60.), np.deg2rad(upper/60.)
    width = 2*np.sin((a+b)/2)*np.sin((b-a)/2)
    return (np.polynomial.legendre.legval(np.cos(a), primitive)
            - np.polynomial.legendre.legval(np.cos(b), primitive))/width

The annulus mean uses the Legendre antiderivative (legint), so a bin is the exact solid-angle average, not w at a bin centre. Because μ1 − μ2 = cos a − cos b = 2 sin((a+b)/2) sin((b−a)/2), the width line avoids cancellation at small angles. The file's self_test checks a pure dipole analytically, and random spectra against 512-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature, to 2 × 10−7.

χ², the jackknife covariance and the Hartlap factor

Only bins above the paper's angular cut enter: θ ≥ 10′ for Planck (8 bins) and θ ≥ 100.25 = 1.78′ for ACT (11 bins). The statistic is χ² = (m − d)T Ĉ−1 (m − d), with the data's jackknife covariance Ĉ, model annulus means m and data d. The model has no free parameters, so the p-value uses all nbins degrees of freedom. A covariance estimated from a finite number of samples gives a biased inverse. The Hartlap correction multiplies Ĉ−1 by h = (N − p − 2)/(N − 1), with N the number of samples (here jackknife samples, one per FRB) and p the number of bins.

            inv = np.linalg.inv(obs["covariance"][np.ix_(use, use)])
            chi2 = lambda m: float((m[use] - obs["w"][use]) @ inv @ (m[use] - obs["w"][use]))
            n = int(use.sum())
            line = "%s (%d bins, Hartlap %.3f):  w=0 %.1f" % (title, n, hartlap[plane], chi2(np.zeros_like(obs["w"])))
            ax.axvspan(1, fs.PAPER_CUT[plane], color="0.92", lw=0, zorder=0)
            for yk, dk, lab, col, ls in pairs:
                cl = S["cl_y_{}__dm_{}_{}".format(yk, dk, plane)]
                ax.plot(theta, fs.angular_correlation(cl, theta, fs.BEAMS[plane]) / 1e-5, color=col, ls=ls, lw=1.2, zorder=4)
                c2 = chi2(fs.annular_correlation(cl, obs["lo"], obs["hi"], fs.BEAMS[plane]))
                line += ";  %s %.1f -> %.1f (p = %.3f)" % (lab.split(" $")[0].split(" y")[0], c2, hartlap[plane] * c2,
                                                           chi2_dist.sf(hartlap[plane] * c2, n))

h = (71 − 8 − 2)/70 = 0.871 for Planck and (31 − 11 − 2)/30 = 0.600 for ACT. The CSV tables and the figure text boxes give χ² without h; the caption file gives both. Jackknife samples are not independent draws, so the Hartlap factor is an approximation here. It multiplies every χ² of a survey by the same number, so it never changes the ranking of models, but it scales both the absolute χ² and the Δχ² between models (ACT: 14.0 becomes 8.4).

Figure 2.9 shows why the model is the ensemble mean over sightlines and not one realisation of 71 or 31 FRBs, and why the data covariance, not the model, carries the sample variance.

2.8 The pressure side: y power against FLAMINGO, and which halos carry it

Five NSIDE 4096 y maps were painted on idark in one catalogue pass on 2026-09-30: Battaglia12, Lee22 as printed, Lee22 × (1+z)2, the hybrid, and the calibrated-range map. All use XGPaint's projected profile with a 4 R200c aperture. Lee22's printed pressure needs only a factor (1+z)2, with no constant: FP = Pprinted (1+z)2 / Psim, the corrected fit divided by the simulated pressure and averaged over 0.04–1.34 R200c, is 0.99–1.07 in CAMELS-TNG (Lee22 doc §3; Section 1). Because the pressure shape is unchanged, that factor and the hybrid switch are per-halo weights applied to the painted profiles:

function map_weights(name::AbstractString, m::Float64, z::Float64)
    name == "b12" && return (1.0, 0.0)
    name == "lee22p" && return (0.0, 1.0)
    name == "lee22p_m1" && return (0.0, (1 + z)^2)
    name == "lee22p_m1_b12hi" && return m < LEE22_FIT_MAX_MSUN ? (0.0, (1 + z)^2) : (1.0, 0.0)
    if name == "lee22p_m1_calib"
        inside = LEE22_FIT_MIN_MSUN <= m < LEE22_FIT_MAX_MSUN && z <= LEE22_FIT_ZMAX
        return inside ? (0.0, (1 + z)^2) : (0.0, 0.0)
    end
    error("unknown map $(name)")
end

Each halo is evaluated once for each base profile (Battaglia12 and Lee22 Table 7), and every map receives wB12 yB12 + wLee22 yLee22. All five maps therefore share geometry, apertures and lookup tables exactly, and differences between them are purely the weights. LEE22_FIT_MAX_MSUN = 1014.8/0.68 = 9.28 × 1014 M☉ (line 34) is the hybrid threshold. The 79,302 halos at z < 0.1 (Lee22 doc §5) are painted from exact per-halo radial tables built with XGPaint.fill_profile_slice! (ExactTable, lines 63-71) instead of the production lookup table, whose redshift spacing Δz = 0.0196 is too coarse where θ200 ∝ 1/dA.

XGPaint edits and behaviours behind the y maps

(i) Edit: the Battaglia12 outer exponent (9f37b1e merged as b924ab1; fixed in e67a8a1). Upstream used generalized_nfw = x̄^γ (1 + x̄^α)^((β − γ)/α) with β = γ − α β, which gives the correct outer factor (1 + x̄)−4.35. Commit 9f37b1e changed the shared exponent to −(β+γ)/α (needed for the Battaglia16 density above) but set β = −γ − αβ for Battaglia12, which turns the outer factor into (1 + x̄)+βraw ≈ (1 + x̄)+4.35, a pressure that grows outwards. The merge b924ab1 carried this into the fork's parametrised get_params, and commit e67a8a1 (2026-04-30) fixed it with β = αβraw − γ. The current code is:

function get_params(model::Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = powerlaw_value(model.P0, m, z₁)
	xc = powerlaw_value(model.x_c, m, z₁)
	α = powerlaw_value(model.alpha, m, z₁)
	β_raw = powerlaw_value(model.beta, m, z₁)
    γ = powerlaw_value(model.gamma, m, z₁)
    # Battaglia parameterizes the outer factor as (1 + x^α)^(-β_raw), while
    # generalized_nfw expects the asymptotic outer slope directly. Matching the
    # large-radius behavior x^(γ - αβ_raw) = x^(-β) gives β = αβ_raw - γ.
    β = α * β_raw - γ
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

The bug is in none of the maps used here (Lee22 doc §1): the local copy of the Battaglia12 lookup table equals the correct-sign profile, the buggy sign would change y by factors of 1029–1040, and the maps' Battaglia12 / FLAMINGO power ratio is 0.70–0.80. (The idark copy of the table that painted the maps was not read; the last two facts are what rule the bug out there.) (ii) Behaviour worked around, not an edit: XGPaint hard-codes Pe = 0.5176 Pth (P_e_los, src/profiles_y.jl:128, also upstream). Lee22 fits Pe directly, so the project wrapper returns P₀=T(p.P0 / LEE22_PRESSURE_ELECTRON_TO_THERMAL) together with β=T(p.alpha * p.beta - p.gamma) (frb_map_generation/lee2022_tsz_pressure_profile.jl:47-51). (iii) Edit used as is: fill_profile_slice! (src/profiles.jl:60) is a fork addition from e67a8a1. The low-z repaint calls it unchanged, at each halo's exact mass and redshift. The repaint changes the Battaglia12 power by −19, −8, −4, −1 and −0.1 % at ℓ = 10, 20, 30, 50 and 100, and not at all above ℓ = 300, as predicted to 10−4 (Lee22 doc §5). (iv) ce1d239 (2026-07-13) relaxed the line-of-sight quadrature tolerance from rtol=eps() to rtol=1e-12 (effect ≤ 7 × 10−14) and lowered some profile_grid default sizes (NlogM 256 → 128, Nlogθ 512 → 256, log Mmin 11 → 12). The y maps load pre-built lookup tables rather than rebuilding them, so the grid defaults do not enter. The 2026-09-29 audit compared the local copies of these tables (Battaglia12 built 2026-04-29, Lee22 built 2026-09-17) with today's code and found agreement to 6.8 × 10−14 (Battaglia12) and bit for bit (Lee22) (A_xgpaint_audit README §1). Figure 2.13 confirms that the maps match an exact per-halo sum over the catalogue, so the ratios to FLAMINGO describe the profiles, not the painting. The (1+z)2 correction fixes the ℓ shape of Lee22's spectrum. Without it, the printed pressure is 2.1–2.3 times FLAMINGO at ℓ ≈ 100–300 and 0.4 times at ℓ = 5000, because high-redshift halos, which dominate small scales, are under-pressured by (1+z)−2. With it, the amplitude is 2.3–3 times FLAMINGO at all ℓ. Two facts locate the excess. The hybrid, which swaps only the roughly 1,200 halos above 1014.8 h−1 M☉ for Battaglia12 (Lee22 doc §5), removes 92, 85, 59 and 39 % of the excess at ℓ = 300, 1000, 3000 and 5000. And the calibrated-range map alone is already 1.4–1.7 times FLAMINGO at ℓ ≥ 3000, although its 1014–1014.8 h−1 M☉ part is also beyond Lee22's fitted bins.

The mass split in Figure 2.14 comes from the exact catalogue-weighted halo model of the 2026-09-29 verification. It takes the (z, M200c) histogram of all 85.2 million painted halos, multiplies by the painted yℓ profiles, and reproduces the maps to 1–3 % at ℓ = 30–3000 (map / model 0.98–1.01 for Battaglia12 and 0.97–1.00 for Lee22; frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_tsz_verification_20260929/B_class_sz/README.md). One-halo power is a sum over halos of |yℓ|2, so it is additive in mass and each band is that mass range's exact share (split, plot_pub_tsz_mass.py:38-42, asserts the bands sum to the total to 10−10). Under Lee22 corrected, halos above 1015 M☉ carry 72 % of the power at ℓ = 300. Lee22 Table 7 has a broken power law in P0, with mass slope 1.08 below and 0.80 above the break mass Mcut = 1013.60 h−1 M☉ (header of lee2022_tsz_pressure_profile.jl), and the fit is applied up to 3.8 × 1015 M☉ = 1015.4 h−1 M☉, roughly 1.4 dex beyond the ~1014 h−1 M☉ end of its fitted bins (the selected mass range in the DM map provenance reaches 3.79 × 1015 M☉). Only FLAMINGO tests the pressure there. FLAMINGO is not TNG, though: its group pressure is about 20 % below TNG's (FP = 1.21–1.25 against FLAMINGO, compared with 0.99–1.07 against CAMELS-TNG; Lee22 doc §3). So the ratio to FLAMINGO mixes model error with simulation differences.

On the FLAMINGO comparison: the HalfDome maps are point-sampled, while FLAMINGO's power is divided by the HEALPix pixel window squared, and both are NSIDE 4096. The y × DM χ² values use the data's jackknife covariance, not FLAMINGO. The tSZ_64k SBI dataset (Section 3) was painted before these corrections, with the coarse low-z lookup tables (Lee22 doc §6). The doc's §6 also lists the kSZ maps as still using the previous density; that is out of date, because the halo kSZ maps were repainted with the corrected density on 2026-10-02 (project commit e8934dc; Section 2.9).

2.9 Halo kSZ against WebSky (brief)

The same densities paint the kinetic SZ (kSZ) effect, the Doppler shift of CMB photons scattering off moving electrons. This is a side topic. A one-halo calculation with WebSky's own halos and velocities, in the same painting engine, reproduces the WebSky halo kSZ spectrum (Stein+20, Fig. 6) to 0.975–1.01 at ℓ = 300–5000 with Battaglia16. With Lee22 corrected the same calculation gives 1.37–1.49 times the WebSky spectrum. The full HalfDome map painted with the WebSky recipe is 1.04–1.20 times WebSky for Battaglia16, and the gap is traced to the velocities, not to XGPaint (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_pub_figures_20261001/captions_ksz.txt; project commit b69b968). The kSZ painter itself is project code (greedy 256-halo blocks, commit 77ac291).

  • Low-DM deficit. The corrected density's fraction of hits below 10 pc cm−3 is 27–41 % below TNG's (TNG has 4.7–6.5 % of its hits there) at every zs. This is the outer profile near R200c, where the fit's shape is least constrained. It is not yet understood.
