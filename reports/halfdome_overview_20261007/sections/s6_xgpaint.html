Appendix: XGPaint edits

This appendix is the reference for what was changed in XGPaint, the Julia halo-painting package behind every tSZ, kSZ and halo-DM product in this project. It lists each commit of the kristero/XGPaint.jl fork on top of WebSky-CITA 29ec48a, shows the code before and after, says why it was changed, which products depend on it and by how much it moves the numbers, measured where possible. It then lists the XGPaint behaviours that were not changed in XGPaint but are handled in project code, so that a reader can tell a library edit from a project-side wrapper.

1. What XGPaint does, and where the fork sits

XGPaint (WebSky-CITA, Zack Li et al.) paints extragalactic foregrounds onto sky maps. For halo profiles the chain is always the same. A profile type (for example Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile or BattagliaTauProfile) returns, for a halo of mass M200c at redshift z, the line-of-sight (LOS) integral of a 3-D profile at an angle θ from the halo centre. profile_grid tabulates that function on a grid in (log θ, z, log10M); build_interpolator fits a cubic B-spline to the logarithm of the table (a LogInterpolatorProfile); the painter loops over halos and adds the interpolated value to every pixel closer than θmax to the halo, by default 4 θ200c. Here M200c and R200c are the mass and radius of the sphere whose mean density is 200 times the critical density ρcrit(z), θ200c = atan(R200c/dA) is its angular radius at angular-diameter distance dA, and x = r/R200c is the radius in units of R200c.

Both profiles used here are generalized NFW (gNFW) shapes, a double power law with an inner slope γ, a core radius xc, a transition sharpness α and an outer slope. Battaglia et al. (2012, "B12") fit the thermal pressure; Battaglia (2016, "B16", the "AGN" fit) fits the gas density. Lee et al. (2022, "Lee22") fit electron density and pressure profiles of the same gNFW form to halos of the IllustrisTNG (TNG) hydrodynamical simulation; the project paints them through XGPaint with wrappers (Section 4.5). The electron pressure gives the Compton parameter y of the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect, the electron column gives the Thomson optical depth τ of the kinetic SZ (kSZ) effect and the dispersion measure (DM) of a fast radio burst (FRB):

gNFW shape: f(x) = (x/xc)γ [1 + (x/xc)α]−β′   (β′ = the outer exponent as printed by Battaglia et al. 2012 and Lee22; for B16 the correct exponent is −(β+γ)/α, Section 4.3)
y(θ) = (σT/mec2) ∫ Pe dl,   τ(θ) = σT ∫ ne dl,   DM(θ) = ∫ ne dl / (1+z)   (ne, Pe and dl physical)
ne2d = ∫ ne dl (XGPaint's projected electron column),   ne = ρgas/mper e,   mper e = me + 2mp/(1+XH) = 1.1369 mp

Here σT is the Thomson cross-section, mec2 the electron rest energy and mp the proton mass. XH = 0.76 is the hydrogen mass fraction, so mper e is the gas mass per free electron of fully ionized hydrogen and helium, and fb = Ωb/Ωm is the cosmic baryon fraction (0.158 for the cosmology of the FRB, Lee22 and tSZ painters, Ωb = 0.049, Ωc = 0.261, h = 0.68; 0.1573 for the HalfDome lightcone cosmology Ωb = 0.0486, Ωc = 0.2603, h = 0.6774 used by the kSZ maps of Figures 6.3 and 6.8). XGPaint integrates every LOS to 105 R200c, so the painted signal is a projection of the whole profile, cut only on the sky at θmax. The project calls this "projected" truncation, in contrast with the "spherical" (chord-limited) truncation of Section 4.4.

3. The fork edits, one by one

3.1 f16a7f3 (2026-02-09): a parametrized physical model

Upstream hard-codes the Battaglia fit coefficients inside get_params, so the only way to change a profile was to edit the package. The tSZ simulation-based inference needs the opposite: nine B12 coefficients drawn from a prior for every row of a dataset. f16a7f3 introduces a three-number container for a mass- and redshift-dependent power law and gives every Battaglia profile type fifteen keyword arguments (amplitude, mass slope and redshift slope for each of P0, xc, α, β, γ), with defaults equal to the published numbers.

"""
    PowerLawParam(amp, alpha_m, alpha_z)

Container for a redshift- and mass-dependent parameter of the form:

```
amp * m^alpha_m * (1 + z)^alpha_z
```
"""
struct PowerLawParam{T}
    amp::T
    alpha_m::T
    alpha_z::T
end

@inline powerlaw_value(p::PowerLawParam{T}, m, z1) where {T} = p.amp * m^p.alpha_m * z1^p.alpha_z

Each parameter is p = amp · mαm · (1+z)αz with m = M200c/1014 M☉. get_params now evaluates five of these instead of literals. Note that the argument is (1+z), passed as z1, exactly as in Battaglia's tables.

src/model.jl l.8-23 (new); src/profiles_y.jl l.27-61 and l.73-98, src/profiles_tau.jl l.28-72 (constructors and get_params). Before: P₀ = 18.1 * m^0.154 * z₁^-0.758 inside a generic get_params(::AbstractGNFW{T}, …). After: P₀ = powerlaw_value(model.P0, m, z₁) inside get_params(model::Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile{T}, …). The first change is units. A column density is ∫ n dl. If both density and path length are comoving, ncom = nphys/(1+z)3 and dlcom = (1+z) dlphys, so the comoving column is the physical column divided by (1+z)2. The Thomson optical depth is σT times the physical column, because σT is a physical cross-section and every electron along the path scatters once. Upstream therefore returned τ too small by (1+z)2. The second change is fb. Battaglia (2016) prints its fit as ρgas = ρcrit ρfit; taken literally, with ρ0 = 4000 at 1014 M☉, that gas density is 1/fb too high, and the gas density needs the factor fb. The fork's comment says "mistake in battaglia 2016: need f_b to convert from m to gas". The project later confirmed this independently: Battaglia's own Fig. A1 is reproduced by its Table A1 only with ρgas = fb ρcrit ρfit (Lee22 calibration document, frb_map_generation/lee22_tsz_corrected/doc/lee22_calibration.html l.95).

Whether fb belongs in the density was tested at the power-spectrum level on 2026-09-19, because one reading of Lee22's Fig. 5 suggested removing it. Without fb the map has 14.96 μK2 at ℓ = 1000, excluded by CLASS-SZ and by the WebSky halo curve (0.199 μK2) alike.

3.3 The gNFW exponent convention, and the Battaglia12 sign bug of March-April 2026

The same Adrian commit also rewrote the exponent of the shared shape function. XGPaint uses one generalized_nfw for the B12 pressure and the B16 density, and each profile's get_params converts its printed β into whatever "β" that function expects. Upstream wrote the outer factor as [1+x̄α](β−γ)/α and stored the B16 β with a minus sign; Adrian's version writes it as [1+x̄α]−(β+γ)/α with the B16 β positive, as printed (Battaglia's printed exponent is −(β−γ)/α, the second B16 typo; see Section 4.3).

function generalized_nfw(x, xc, α, β, γ)
    x̄ = x / xc
    return x̄^γ * (1 + x̄^α)^((β - γ) / α)
end

function _generalized_scaled_nfw(x̄, α, β, γ)
    return x̄^γ * (1 + x̄^α)^((β - γ) / α)
end


function get_params(::AbstractGNFW{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = 18.1 * m^0.154 * z₁^-0.758
	xc = 0.497 * m^-0.00865 * z₁^0.731
	β = 4.35 * m^0.0393 * z₁^0.415
	α = 1
    γ = -0.3
    β = γ - α * β  # Sigurd's conversion from Battaglia to standard NFW
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

Upstream. With the B12 conversion β = γ − αβB the exponent (β−γ)/α equals −βB, so the outer factor is [1+x̄]−βB as in Battaglia et al. (2012). For B16 (profiles_tau.jl) upstream stores β = −3.83, and (β−γ)/α = −(3.83 − 0.2)/0.88, which is already the corrected −(β+γ)/α.

function generalized_nfw(x, xc, α, β, γ)
    x̄ = x / xc
    return x̄^γ * (1 + x̄^α)^(-(β + γ) / α)    # correction to battaglia 2016 tau.
end

function _generalized_scaled_nfw(x̄, α, β, γ)
    return x̄^γ * (1 + x̄^α)^(-(β + γ) / α)
end


function get_params(model::Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = powerlaw_value(model.P0, m, z₁)
	xc = powerlaw_value(model.x_c, m, z₁)
	α = powerlaw_value(model.alpha, m, z₁)
	β_raw = powerlaw_value(model.beta, m, z₁)
    γ = powerlaw_value(model.gamma, m, z₁)
    # Battaglia parameterizes the outer factor as (1 + x^α)^(-β_raw), while
    # generalized_nfw expects the asymptotic outer slope directly. Matching the
    # large-radius behavior x^(γ - αβ_raw) = x^(-β) gives β = αβ_raw - γ.
    β = α * β_raw - γ
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

The rewrite is a re-labelling only if every caller adapts. Adrian's commit adapted B16 (β = +3.83) but converted B12 with β = −γ − αβB, which gives −(β+γ)/α = +βB: a pressure that grows as (1+x̄)+4.35 at large radius. 3.4 e67a8a1 (2026-04-30): the 0.9, the FRB profile, profile slices, threaded tables and a positivity floor

This is the largest fork commit ("FRB + tSZ adapted for cluster testing/parallelizing", 448 lines added). Besides the B12 sign fix it does six things.

(a) A factor 0.9 in ne2d. return 0.9 * result / factor (fork profiles_tau.jl l.106). It acts as a free-electron fraction ffree = 0.9 applied on top of full ionization; the commit does not say what it is meant to represent or where it comes from. It lowers every B16 τ and DM by 10 % and the kSZ power by 19 %. CLASS-SZ has a parameter of the same meaning (f_free, default 1.0), which the project sets to 0.9 in its comparisons to mirror XGPaint; with that setting the two B16 gas densities agree to 0.4 % and the electron conversions differ by 0.3 % in τ (Section 4.2). With the 0.9, XGPaint's electron density is ne = 0.9 ρgas/mper e = 0.7916 ρgas/mp, against WebSky's (1 − Yp/2) ρgas/mp = 0.88 ρgas/mp (Yp = 0.24, the helium mass fraction, with full ionization and no 0.9); that difference alone is a factor 0.809 in the kSZ power budget against the WebSky halo curve (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_ksz_debug_20261002/synthesis/budget.txt).

(b) profiles_frb.jl, the FRB DM profile. An AbstractFRBProfile is a τ profile whose evaluation returns the observed DM instead of τ. It reuses rho_2d and ne2d unchanged, so it inherits fb, the physical column and the 0.9.

"""
    compute_DM(model, r, M_200c, z)

Compute the observed halo DM contribution in `pc/cm^3` directly from the
projected electron column density.
"""
function compute_DM(model::AbstractFRBProfile{T}, r, M_200c, z) where T
    electron_column = ustrip(u"m^-2", ne2d(model, r, M_200c, z))
    return T(electron_column * M2_TO_PC_CM3 / (one(z) + z))
end

# direct evaluation follows the rest of the profile API: mass is in Msun and
# the returned value is a plain number in pc/cm^3

DM = ne2d/(1+z), converted from m-2 to pc cm-3. The 1/(1+z) is the cosmological factor of the observed DM: the dispersion delay accrued at the halo scales as 1/ν2 with ν there (1+z) times the observed frequency, and the delay is then stretched by (1+z) on the way to the observer, a net 1/(1+z). The constant M2_TO_PC_CM3 on l.3 is the subject of 71646e2 (Section 3.5).

function fill_profile_slice!(dest, model::AbstractGNFW{T}, logthetas, mass, redshift) where T
    prepared = prepare_profile_slice(model, mass, redshift)
    @inbounds for itheta in eachindex(logthetas)
        theta = exp(logthetas[itheta])
        dest[itheta] = max(zero(T), evaluate_profile_slice(model, prepared, theta, mass, redshift))
    end
    return nothing
end

    redshifts = LinRange(z_min, z_max, N_z)
    logMs = LinRange(logM_min, logM_max, N_logM)
    return profile_grid(model, logthetas, redshifts, logMs)
end

(e) Threaded tables. The table loop runs over the flattened (z, M) index in up to 8 × nthreads chunks instead of one chunk of masses per thread, and the beam transform and negative-value cleanup are threaded over the same flattened (z, M) index (one chunk per thread). (f) A positivity floor and a finite check. build_interpolator takes the logarithm of the table. 4.1 The linear-z table grid fails at low redshift

The tables are tabulated on redshifts = LinRange(z_min, z_max, N_z) with zmin = 0.001, zmax = 5 and Nz = 256 (upstream and fork alike), a node spacing Δz = 0.0196. The interpolator works at fixed θ, but a halo's angular size scales as 1/dA, which changes by a factor of 20 inside the first cell (z = 0.001 to 0.0206). At fixed θ the profile value at the two bracketing nodes then refers to radii a factor 20 apart, and the spline in z cannot represent it. The audit's first pass tested z = 0.1-2 only and found the painted tables within 8.9×10-5 (B12) and 6.0×10-4 (Lee22) of an independent LOS integral (Figure 6.7). The verifier (an independent re-check of audit A, frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_tsz_verification_20260929/verify_A_xgpaint_audit/README.md) then extended the test down to z = 0.0022 (11 masses, x = 0.02-3.9) and found the error (Figure 6.5). Below z = 0.01 the interpolated y at x ≥ 1 is too high by 10 % up to a factor of about 35 (|yitp/ydirect − 1| from 0.1 to 34) and by up to 9 % at x = 0.1; at z = 0.01, still inside the first table cell, |yitp/ydirect − 1| at x ≥ 1 reaches 8.5 for Lee22 and 4.0 for B12; between z = 0.02 and 0.05 it is at most 5 % for B12 and 7 % for Lee22 at x ≥ 1, the largest values at x = 3.9 for the most massive halos (M ≥ 1014.9 M☉) (v1c_itp_vs_direct_grid.csv). 850 catalogue halos lie in the first cell, z < 0.0206. The stored tables themselves are correct (within 2.8×10-8 of an independent integral at their nodes); the error is interpolation between nodes. On the map it shows only at the largest scales (Figure 6.6): compared with re-evaluating the 79,302 halos at z < 0.1 exactly, the stored B12 y power (Cℓ averaged over ℓ ± 5 before the ratio is taken) is too high by 16 %, 10 %, 4 %, 1.2 % and 0.08 % at ℓ = 10, 20, 30, 50, 100 and by less than 10-3 above ℓ = 100 (Lee22: 3.7 %, 2.9 %, 1.0 %, 0.2 % and −0.07 %), and the mean y is too high by 0.54 % (B12) and 0.43 % (Lee22). For the B16 kSZ map (degraded to NSIDE 512, halos at z < 0.05 corrected) the stored-to-corrected power ratio is 1.18 at ℓ = 20-40, 1.04 at ℓ = 41-81 and within 1.1×10-3 of 1 at ℓ = 82-660 (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_ksz_debug_20261002/verify_B1/step4_map_level.txt). NSIDE is the HEALPix resolution parameter; a map has 12 NSIDE2 pixels.

The fix is in the painters, not in XGPaint. For halos below a threshold (z < 0.1) the y painter and the kSZ painter build a radial table for that one halo at its exact mass and redshift with XGPaint's own slice functions (the y painter calls fill_profile_slice!, the function that fills the table nodes; the kSZ painter calls prepare_profile_slice/evaluate_profile_slice directly), and interpolate it in log θ only. Halos at z ≥ 0.1 go through the production tables exactly as before, so those pixels are reproduced bit for bit.

"""Per-halo radial table: log-theta nodes from max(theta_min, 1e-5 theta200) to theta_max, step `dlog`."""
struct ExactTable
    logtheta::Vector{Float64}
    y::Vector{Float64}
end

function ExactTable(model, mass, z, theta_min, theta200, theta_max; dlog=0.02)
    lo = log(max(theta_min, 1.0e-5 * theta200))
    hi = log(theta_max) + 1.0e-9
    n = max(8, ceil(Int, (hi - lo) / dlog) + 1)
    logtheta = collect(range(lo, hi, length=n))
    y = zeros(n)
    XGPaint.fill_profile_slice!(y, model, logtheta, mass, z)
    return ExactTable(logtheta, y)
end

function (t::ExactTable)(theta)
    lt = clamp(log(theta), first(t.logtheta), last(t.logtheta))
    k = clamp(searchsortedlast(t.logtheta, lt), 1, length(t.logtheta) - 1)
    f = (lt - t.logtheta[k]) / (t.logtheta[k + 1] - t.logtheta[k])
    y0, y1 = t.y[k], t.y[k + 1]
    return (y0 > 0 && y1 > 0) ? exp((1 - f) * log(y0) + f * log(y1)) : (1 - f) * y0 + f * y1
end

Nodes every 0.02 in ln θ from max(θmin, 10-5 θ200c) to the aperture edge (about 650 nodes for a 4 θ200c aperture), filled by XGPaint itself, so the exact table carries no new physics code. The painter's self-test requires agreement with fill_profile_slice! at random angles to 2×10-3 for 300 low-z halos (paint_tsz_variants.jl l.139-159). The kSZ painter does the same with 400 fixed nodes in _exact_table (ksz/paint_ksz_websky.jl l.88-102).

The tSZ 64k dataset avoids the problem differently: its engine never calls build_interpolator and tabulates z on a logarithmic grid, wrapping the spline so that it is queried in ln z.

"""Vary node counts, never the angular/mass/redshift domain or LOS tolerance."""
function build_cache(cfg, nodes)
    model = ChordMeanProfile(build_tsz_model(cfg), 4.)
    rft = XGPaint.RadialFourierTransform(n=512, pad=cfg.interpolator_pad)
    lt = LinRange(log(minimum(rft.r)), log(maximum(rft.r)), nodes[1])
    lz = LinRange(log(.001), log(5.), nodes[2])
    lm = LinRange(12., cfg.interpolator_logM_max, nodes[3])

    @assert all(isfinite, values) && minimum(values) >= 0
    values .= max.(values, 1e-300)
    interpolator = XGPaint.Interpolations.interpolate(log.(values),
        XGPaint.BSpline(XGPaint.Cubic(XGPaint.Line(XGPaint.OnGrid()))))
    scaled = XGPaint.scale(interpolator, lt, lz, lm)
    return XGPaint.LogInterpolatorProfile(model, LogRedshiftGrid(scaled, (lt, lz, lm)))
end

4.2 Battaglia16 electron conventions: the 0.9 and the mass per electron

XGPaint's ne2d turns a gas density into electrons with two numbers: mper e = me + 2mp/(1+XH) (upstream, full ionization of H and He at XH = 0.76) and the factor 0.9 (fork, e67a8a1). Neither was changed again. Every place where another convention is needed compensates in project code instead.

  • Lee22 density. Lee22's fit is already an electron density, ne = n0 f(x) n200 with n200 = 200 ρcrit fb/(XHmp). To pass it through ne2d unchanged, the wrapper sets P0 = N n0 200 mper e/(0.9 XH mp), which cancels both XGPaint numbers exactly (Section 4.5). The earlier production reading, normalization=:xgpaint_ne2d with P0 = 200 n0, let them act and so painted 0.9 XHmp/mper e = 0.6016 times the printed density (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_fig5_audit_20260922/REPORT.md).
  • WebSky recipe. The "WebSky-recipe" kSZ variant is a project painter that reproduces the halo-kSZ prescription of the WebSky simulation (Stein et al. 2020). WebSky uses ne = (1 − Yp/2) ρgas/mp with Yp = 0.24. The WebSky-recipe kSZ variant multiplies the XGPaint B16 τ by WS_NE / XGP_NE = 0.88/0.7916 = 1.112 (ksz/paint_ksz_websky.jl l.27-28, l.83).
  • CLASS-SZ. The comparisons run class_sz with f_free = 0.9 to mirror XGPaint's 0.9. Its B16 gas density agrees with XGPaint's P0 f(x) fb ρcrit to 0.4 % at all tested radii, and its fixed μe = 1.14 (mean gas mass per electron in units of mp) against XGPaint's 1.137 is a further 0.3 % in τ (truncation_comparison/TSZ_KSZ_TRUNCATION_COMPARISON_20260917.md).

4.3 The printed Battaglia16 typos

Battaglia (2016) prints the density fit with two errors: ρgas = ρcrit ρfit without fb, and the exponent −(β−γ)/α instead of −(β+γ)/α. The paper's own Fig. A1 is reproduced by its Table A1 only with both corrected; Lee22 copied the printed formula, which is why their drawn "Battaglia (2016)" curve is 1/fb = 6.4 times too high and tilted (lee22_calibration.html l.95). The kSZ debug run reached the same conclusion by comparing B16's tabulated fit with curves digitized from its Fig. 5: at the profile peak the table-based profile is 0.98-1.07 of the drawn curves with −(β+γ)/α and 0.75-0.83 with the printed −(β−γ)/α (frb_map_generation/outputs/lee22_ksz_debug_20261002/tau_amplitude/b16_fig5_convention_check.txt). The two corrections have different histories in XGPaint. The exponent was already correct in upstream (stored β = −3.83 with (β−γ)/α, Section 3.3); only its bookkeeping was rewritten. The fb was missing in upstream and is a fork edit (9f37b1e, Section 3.2). No project code changes either.

4.4 Projected line of sight against a 3-D sphere: the chord-mean wrapper

XGPaint integrates each LOS to 105 R200c and cuts only the disc on the sky. A ray at projected radius x < 4 therefore also collects gas at 3-D radii beyond 4 R200c. CLASS-SZ, and the simulation measurements the FRB work compares with, truncate at a sphere. The FRB generator was switched to a chord-limited LOS on 2026-09-16, and on 2026-09-17 the same construction was provided for tSZ and kSZ as a wrapper type, ChordMeanProfile, that plugs into XGPaint's table builder through the slice functions of Section 3.4.

"""Exact chord factor 2 L_max at angle theta given theta_max = X theta200c."""
@inline function chord_factor(theta, theta_max, X)
    ratio = theta / theta_max
    ratio >= 1 && return 0.0
    return 2X * sqrt(1 - ratio^2)
end

# XGPaint's profile_grid calls prepare_profile_slice once per (M, z) and
# evaluate_profile_slice per theta; both are extended for this type.
function XGPaint.prepare_profile_slice(m::ChordMeanProfile, mass, z)
    inner = m.inner
    Mu = mass * XGPaint.M_sun
    par = XGPaint.get_params(inner, Mu, z)
    θ200 = theta_r200c(inner, mass, z)
    xc, α, β, γ = Float64(par.xc), Float64(par.α), Float64(par.β), Float64(par.γ)
    A = projection_amplitude(inner, mass, z, θ200, xc, α, β, γ)
    return (; θ200, xc, α, β, γ, A, X=m.sphere)
end

@inline function XGPaint.evaluate_profile_slice(::ChordMeanProfile, p, theta, mass, z)
    x = Float64(theta) / p.θ200
    return p.A * chord_mean(x, p.xc, p.α, p.β, p.γ, p.X)
end

The table stores the chord mean g = (integral along the chord inside the sphere)/(2L), L = √(X2 − x2), which stays positive and smooth up to the sphere edge, so XGPaint's log-spline still works. The painter multiplies g by the exact chord factor 2X√(1 − (θ/θmax)2), which sends the signal to zero at the edge. The amplitude is taken from XGPaint itself (projection_amplitude, l.91-98: the prepared slice amplitude for B12, XGPaint's own value over its infinite LOS integral for τ), so no normalization is re-derived. Self-test: chord mean times chord factor equals direct chord quadrature to 10-8; tables equal direct evaluation to 10-7.

At the profile level the sphere keeps 0.81-0.84 of B12's projected y at x = 3 and 0.30-0.33 at x = 3.9, and 0.972-0.991 of the disc-integrated signal; for the shallower B16 density it keeps 0.68-0.74 and 0.23-0.26 at those radii and 0.875-0.935 of the disc. On the full-lightcone maps (Figures 6.8 and 6.9) the fix lowers the y power by at most 1.2 % (ℓ < 50) and by nothing above ℓ ≈ 2000, and lowers the halo kSZ power by 11-13 % below ℓ = 400, 8 % at ℓ ≈ 800, 4 % at ℓ ≈ 2100 and 1.7 % at ℓ ≈ 3400. The tSZ 64k dataset uses the same wrapper (engine copy with a bounded, checked quadrature: tSZ_64k_flat_prior_SO_baseline_deproj0/engine/spherical_truncation_profiles.jl), as do the kSZ products since 2026-09-17.

4.5 Lee22 through XGPaint: two get_params wrappers

Lee22's fits use broken power laws in mass (different slopes below and above a pivot mass Mcut) that a PowerLawParam cannot represent. Rather than editing XGPaint, the project defines new profile types that supply only XGPaint.get_params (and, for tSZ, the slice functions), so XGPaint's LOS integral, rho_2d/ne2d, compute_DM, tables and painter run unchanged.

function XGPaint.get_params(model::Lee2022XGPaintDMProfile{T}, M_200c, z) where {T}
    mass_msun = Float64(M_200c / XGPaint.M_sun)
    redshift = Float64(z)
    pars = lee2022_parameters(model.lee, mass_msun, redshift)
    beta = pars.alpha * pars.beta_prime - pars.gamma
    zfac = lee2022_redshift_scaling_factor(model.lee, redshift)
    if model.lee.normalization == :xgpaint_ne2d
        # XGPaint-native: the dimensionless Lee22 profile is the gas density in units of 200 f_b rho_cr;
        # no Lee22-side hydrogen factor, XGPaint's ne2d supplies the electron conversion.
        p0 = 200 * pars.n0 * zfac
    else
        mp_kg = Float64(ustrip(uconvert(u"kg", XGPaint.constants.ProtonMass)))
        p0 = lee2022_normalization_factor(model.lee) * zfac * pars.n0 * 200 * xgpaint_mass_per_electron_kg() /
             (XGPAINT_NE2D_GAS_FRACTION * model.lee.hydrogen_mass_fraction * mp_kg)
    end
    return (xc=T(pars.x_c), α=T(pars.alpha), β=T(beta), γ=T(pars.gamma), P₀=T(p0))
end

Two conversions do all the work. β = αβ′ − γ maps Lee22's printed exponent onto XGPaint's −(β+γ)/α (Section 3.3), and P0 cancels XGPaint's 0.9 and mper e and supplies the 1/(XHmp) of Lee22's n200, times the normalization reading N and the redshift-scaling factor (for the final products the derived (hTNG(1+z))3XH2). The :xgpaint_ne2d branch is the superseded reading that let XGPaint's constants act.

The tSZ wrapper (frb_map_generation/lee2022_tsz_pressure_profile.jl l.47-51) passes P0/0.5176 because XGPaint multiplies the thermal pressure by 0.5176 (= μ/μe) to get the electron pressure while Lee22 fits Pe directly, and β′ + 0.3 for the exponent. The kSZ code reuses the DM wrapper as a τ profile (Lee22TauProfile in ksz/ksz_models.jl). The equivalence was checked at two levels (frb_map_generation/LIKE_FOR_LIKE_SPHERICAL_R200C_20260916.md l.46). XGPaint's projected chain (rho_2d, ne2d, compute_DM with its infinite LOS) was exercised by the self-test: compute_DM on the wrapper matches the Lee22 code's projected column to 2×10-6 and its 3-D density to 10-9 at five (M, z, x) points for both fits. The FRB histograms of Figure 6.10 were made in spherical mode, where the wrapper's parameters are fed to the project's chord quadrature (Battaglia16DensityDMProfile, generate_halfdome_z1_dm_mass_windows.jl l.200), the same quadrature the Lee22-code products use; there they equal the Lee22-code products in 300 of 300 bins, means to 2×10-16. That comparison therefore tests the get_params mapping (β and P0), not XGPaint's LOS integral.

4.6 kSZ painter threading

The halo kSZ maps are painted by project code, not by XGPaint's paint!. On 2026-10-04 the painter adopted the scheduling of the accelerated tSZ painter: halos are handed out in blocks of 256 to whichever thread is free through an atomic counter, because the catalogue is sorted by decreasing redshift and the large low-z discs at its end would otherwise pile up on one thread.

"""Greedy block worker: claims the next block of BLOCK halos until none is left; returns its own counters."""
function paint_worker(c::PaintContext, next::Threads.Atomic{Int}, nblk, m200c, m200m, redshifts, positions, vlos_over_c, sel, mws)
    n = 0; ncut = 0; nexact = 0; N = length(m200c)
    while true
        b = Threads.atomic_add!(next, 1)            # returns the previous value
        b > nblk && break
        for i in (b - 1) * BLOCK + 1:min(b * BLOCK, N)
            a, s, e = paint_halo!(c, i, m200c, m200m, redshifts, positions, vlos_over_c, sel, mws)
            n += a; ncut += s; nexact += e
        end
    end
    return (n, ncut, nexact)
end

function paint_batch!(c::PaintContext, m200c, m200m, redshifts, positions, vlos_over_c, counters, sel, mws)
    nblk = cld(length(m200c), BLOCK); next = Threads.Atomic{Int}(1)
    tasks = [Threads.@spawn paint_worker(c, next, nblk, m200c, m200m, redshifts, positions, vlos_over_c, sel, mws)
             for _ in 1:Threads.nthreads()]
    for t in tasks
        n, s, e = fetch(t)::Tuple{Int,Int,Int}
        counters[1] += n; counters[2] += s; counters[3] += e
    end
end

Threads.@spawn workers on a shared Threads.Atomic{Int} block counter emulate @threads :greedy, which idark's Julia 1.6 lacks. Pixel writes are protected by one lock per HEALPix ring inside paint_halo!; only the work allocation and the order of floating-point sums change.

Verification (HalfDome commits 77ac291 and 7f5b73a): single-thread output byte-identical to the previous painter, 16 threads within 3.3×10-16, block sizes 1, 256 and 4096 within 1.4×10-16. With the Julia 1.12.2 runtime the full lightcone paints in 527 s (idark job 600626) against 5098 s and 8343 s for the two earlier Julia 1.6 runs, with identical counters (7,090,669,884 pixel updates, 79,302 exact low-z halos) and spectra equal to the production run to 7.4×10-11.

4.7 Other unedited XGPaint behaviours worth knowing

  • Radiation in ρcrit. XGPaint's cosmology includes photons and neutrinos, which raises ρcrit(z) over the Ωm+ΩΛ value by 4×10-5 at z = 0.1, 8×10-4 at z = 2 and 1.1×10-3 at z = 3. y and τ scale with it; the audits remove it when comparing with Lee22's convention (audit A README).
  • Painter cosmology. The FRB, Lee22 and tSZ painters build profiles with Ωb = 0.049, Ωc = 0.261 (Ωm = 0.31), h = 0.68, while the HalfDome lightcone has Ωb = 0.0486, Ωc = 0.2603 (Ωm = 0.3089), h = 0.6774. The truncation-comparison maps of 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-19 (Figures 6.3, 6.8, 6.9; truncation_comparison/paint_ksz_fb_comparison_maps.jl l.19) and the WebSky-recipe kSZ painter (ksz/ksz_models_halfdome.jl l.9) use the lightcone values. This is a choice of arguments, not a library issue.
  • Table range. Tables built with the current defaults span 1012-1015.7 M☉ (the production B12 table 1011-1015.7) and are not extrapolated; the HalfDome catalogue spans 4.46×1012-2.57×1015 M☉/h (TSZ_KSZ_TRUNCATION_COMPARISON_20260917.md), that is 6.6×1012-3.8×1015 M☉ with h = 0.68, so no halo falls outside. Angles below the first table node are clamped to it (compute_θmin, src/profiles.jl l.284), as every painter here does explicitly.
