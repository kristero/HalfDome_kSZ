4. Seed, mask and simulation impact

The 64k tSZ posterior estimator was trained on one HalfDome halo lightcone (simulation seed 100) seen through one sky mask (mask seed 12345). Only the nine pressure-profile parameters of Battaglia et al. (2012, B12) and the Simons Observatory (SO) noise changed from one training row to the next. This section shows what that choice costs. When the same pressure law is painted onto nine other lightcones, or observed through nine other masks, the original Merlin posterior median of P0 moves from about 12 to 45-54 on the other lightcones and to 24-46 through the other masks (the truth is 18.1). Controlled spectral tests trace the shift to the fine multipole pattern of the training sky, which the network learned as if it were pressure information. The best mitigation tested, retraining on 50-multipole bands, removes most of the disagreement between skies. The price is 68% intervals that are 18-25% wider at the Battaglia truth and about 46% wider when averaged over the prior.

4.1 The question: what the training set holds fixed

Simulation-based inference (SBI) replaces an explicit likelihood with simulations. In neural posterior estimation (NPE) one draws parameters θ from the prior, runs the simulator to obtain data x, and trains a conditional density estimator qφ(θ | x) on the pairs. If the network is flexible enough and the simulations are numerous, qφ converges to the posterior p(θ | x) of the simulator that generated the pairs, including every assumption frozen inside that simulator. The project's estimator, Merlin, first whitens each spectrum with the Cholesky factor L of the SO noise covariance (C = LLT, so L-1x has unit noise covariance). It then projects the result onto 83 principal components (PCA) and passes each component through an asinh transform, which is linear within about one noise unit of the centre and logarithmic beyond. A learned compression head reduces this to 18 features, which condition a masked autoregressive flow (MAF, a normalising flow built from autoregressive affine layers). Three independently trained networks are mixed with equal weight. Section 5 describes this chain. Here only the training set matters.

The 64k dataset (tSZ_64k_flat_prior_SO_baseline_deproj0) draws 65,536 parameter vectors from a scrambled Sobol sequence (a quasi-random, low-discrepancy sequence that fills the box more evenly than independent random draws; seed 20260920) that is uniform over the box in Table 4.1. The inference uses 64,440 of these rows: a 61,440-row training pool and 3,000 reserved test rows. Each row has two noise seeds of its own, derived from a SHA-256 hash of the row and split index with master seed 20260920, so no two rows share a noise map. But every row is painted from the same halo catalogue, lightcone_100.hdf5 (85,224,251 halos with M200c ≥ 1012 M⊙), and observed through the same apodised cap mask with seed 12345. The bundle README states the consequence directly: "the fixed mask and single catalogue give conditional-noise spectra, not a full observational covariance." Formally, the network learns

qφ(θ | D) ≃ p(θ | D, H100, W12345),   whereas inference on a new sky needs   p(θ | D) = ∫∫ p(θ, H, W | D) dH dW.

Here θ is the nine-parameter pressure vector, D the beam-corrected pseudo-spectrum (defined in 4.2), H the halo lightcone and W the mask. Why should this matter? The tSZ power spectrum is dominated by rare, massive clusters. A given lightcone contains a particular set of them at particular positions and redshifts. A given mask selects a particular subset and couples neighbouring multipoles. The masked clean spectrum is therefore not a smooth curve. It carries a multipole-to-multipole pattern, a fingerprint of that sky and window. In the training set this fingerprint is the same in every row and is modulated only by θ. The noise varies, so the network learns to average over noise. It has no reason to treat the fingerprint as a nuisance, because inside the training distribution the fingerprint is a deterministic function of θ. A different sky carries a different fingerprint, and nothing prevents the network from reading that change as a change in pressure. The seed/mask programme of 2-6 October 2026 measured how large this effect is, why it acts in a common direction, and what removing it costs.

The physics painted in every sky. Each halo receives a Battaglia et al. (2012, B12) generalised Navarro-Frenk-White (gNFW) pressure profile. B12 fit the thermal gas pressure; XGPaint converts it to electron pressure with a fixed factor:

Pth(r | M, z) / P200(M, z) = P0(M, z) · (x/xc)−0.3 · [1 + x/xc]−β(M, z),    x = r / R200c
P200 = 200 ρcrit(z) fb G M200c / (2 R200c),    fb = Ωb/Ωm,    Pe = 0.5176 Pth
A(M, z) = A0 · (M / 1014 M⊙)αm,A · (1 + z)αz,A,    A ∈ {P0, xc, β}
y(b) = (σT / mec2) ∫ Pe(√(b2 + l2)) dl,    |l| ≤ √((4R200c)2 − b2)

P200 is the self-similar pressure scale of a halo of mass M200c (the mass inside R200c, the radius enclosing a mean density of 200 times the critical density). The factor 0.5176 is hard-coded in XGPaint (P_e_los and the amplitude in prepare_profile_slice, src/profiles_y.jl:128 and :158 at tag validated-tsz256-20260922). It equals 2(XH + 1)/(5XH + 3) for a hydrogen mass fraction XH = 0.76, the electron share of the pressure of fully ionised hydrogen-helium gas. It is the same in every sky and drops out of all seed and mask comparisons. The inner slope is fixed at −0.3 and the transition exponent at 1. Compton-y is the line-of-sight integral of electron pressure at impact parameter b, with σT the Thomson cross-section and mec2 the electron rest energy. The integral is restricted to a sphere of radius 4R200c. Catalogue masses in M⊙/h are divided by h = 0.68, halos above 1012 M⊙ are kept, and all finite, non-negative catalogue redshifts are used (the profile cache spans z = 0.001-5; no low-redshift selection is applied). These choices are identical for all ten lightcones. The halo counts differ, from 85,215,792 (seed 108) to 85,267,919 (seed 102), because the halos themselves differ (SBI_analysis/tsz_seed_offset_diagnosis_20261005/diagnostics.json, contracts).

The observation. The painter samples the y map at the pixel centres of a HEALPix NSIDE 8192 grid, applies a 2-arcmin Gaussian beam in harmonic space and synthesises an NSIDE 4096 map. The map is multiplied by a 40%-sky cap with 60-arcmin cosine apodisation. Two independent SO noise maps are added, and the signed cross-spectrum of the two masked splits is measured. This is a pseudo-Cℓ, computed on the masked sky without fsky division, mask deconvolution or noise-bias subtraction. The analysis uses Dℓ = ℓ(ℓ+1)Cℓ/2π at ℓ = 80-7979 (7,900 multipoles). The trained model expects the beam-consistent combination

Dℓobs = Dℓss + Bℓ (Dℓprod − Dℓss − Dℓnn) + Bℓ2 Dℓnn

Here ss is the masked beam-smoothed clean signal, prod the cross-spectrum of the two signal-plus-noise maps, nn the cross-spectrum of the two noise-only maps and Bℓ the 2-arcmin beam. The middle term is the signal-noise cross power, rescaled to the beam convention of the signal. This decomposition into a clean term, a signal-noise term and a noise-noise term is the basis of the offset diagnosis in 4.5. At a fixed mask the nn term is identical for every lightcone, because the noise maps are fixed.

function random_unit_vector(rng::AbstractRNG)
    z = 2.0 * rand(rng) - 1.0
    phi = 2.0 * pi * rand(rng)
    rxy = sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - z^2))
    return rxy * cos(phi), rxy * sin(phi), z
end

function random_apodized_cap_mask(
    nside::Integer,
    fsky::Real,
    apodization_arcmin::Real,
    rng::AbstractRNG
)
    res = Healpix.Resolution(Int(nside))
    mask = HealpixMap{Float64, RingOrder}(Int(nside))
    fill!(mask.pixels, 0.0)

    cx, cy, cz = random_unit_vector(rng)
    radius = acos(1.0 - 2.0 * Float64(fsky))
    apo = deg2rad(Float64(apodization_arcmin) / 60.0)
    apo = min(max(apo, 0.0), 0.99 * radius)
    inner_radius = radius - apo
    support_count = 0

    @inbounds for pix in eachindex(mask.pixels)
        vx, vy, vz = Healpix.pix2vecRing(res, pix)
        cosang = clamp(cx * vx + cy * vy + cz * vz, -1.0, 1.0)
        angle = acos(cosang)

        if angle <= radius
            support_count += 1
            if apo == 0.0 || angle <= inner_radius
                mask.pixels[pix] = 1.0
            else
                x = (angle - inner_radius) / apo
                mask.pixels[pix] = 0.5 * (1.0 + cos(pi * x))
            end

XGPaint edit relied on here: the nine-parameter Battaglia12 profile (fork commits f16a7f3 and e67a8a1)

No XGPaint source was edited for any of the seed/mask experiments. All ten lightcones were painted by the frozen 64k engine, whose tSZ_64k_flat_prior_SO_baseline_deproj0/julia_env/Manifest.toml:1976-1982 pins kristero/XGPaint.jl at tag validated-tsz256-20260922. In the dev checkout this tag resolves to commit 35f79ad, whose message says its tree is identical to ce1d239, the XGPaint snapshot used by the validated 256-row test. What the study does rely on is that the fork can vary the Battaglia parameters at all. Upstream XGPaint (WebSky-CITA, upstream/main = 29ec48a) hard-codes the B12 numbers inside get_params:

function get_params(::AbstractGNFW{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = 18.1 * m^0.154 * z₁^-0.758
	xc = 0.497 * m^-0.00865 * z₁^0.731
	β = 4.35 * m^0.0393 * z₁^0.415
	α = 1
    γ = -0.3
    β = γ - α * β  # Sigurd's conversion from Battaglia to standard NFW
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

Upstream fixes the nine B12 numbers in the source (lines 45-47), so they cannot be varied row by row. Line 50 stores β = γ − αβraw, and upstream's generalized_nfw (src/profiles_y.jl:34 at 29ec48a) raises (1 + x̄α) to the power (β − γ)/α = −βraw, which is the B12 outer factor.

Fork commit f16a7f3 ("Parametrized physical model", 2026-02-09) added PowerLawParam fields and keyword arguments (P0_amp, P0_alpha_m, …) whose defaults are exactly the B12 values. It kept upstream's convention β = γ − αβraw with exponent (β − γ)/α. The β convention was afterwards broken and later repaired, as follows (all checked in the dev checkout with git log --graph, git merge-base and git show). Adrian Bayer's commit 9f37b1e ("fixing ksz", 2026-02-27, on the upstream WebSky-CITA adrian branch) changed the gNFW exponent to −(β + γ)/α, commented as a correction for the Battaglia 2016 tau profile. In the same commit it changed the tSZ conversion to β = −γ − αβraw. Together these give the tSZ outer factor (1 + x̄α)+βraw, which has the wrong sign. The fork merged that branch into the parametrised line in b924ab1 ("addition of Adrian fixes", 2026-03-02, parents b459728 and 077b849), so the parametrised fork carried the wrong sign from then on. A separate fork commit, 71bc3fd ("Adrian fixes", 2026-03-03), applies the same change on a side branch. It is not in the history of the tag used here. Commit e67a8a1 (2026-04-30) restored the correct outer slope with β = αβraw − γ. It also added the prepare_profile_slice/evaluate_profile_slice hooks that profile_grid calls (src/profiles.jl); the project's sphere wrapper extends these hooks and reads the tSZ amplitude from them. The state used here, after that fix, is:

function get_params(model::Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = powerlaw_value(model.P0, m, z₁)
	xc = powerlaw_value(model.x_c, m, z₁)
	α = powerlaw_value(model.alpha, m, z₁)
	β_raw = powerlaw_value(model.beta, m, z₁)
    γ = powerlaw_value(model.gamma, m, z₁)
    # Battaglia parameterizes the outer factor as (1 + x^α)^(-β_raw), while
    # generalized_nfw expects the asymptotic outer slope directly. Matching the
    # large-radius behavior x^(γ - αβ_raw) = x^(-β) gives β = αβ_raw - γ.
    β = α * β_raw - γ
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

Both versions produce the same outer factor (1 + x̄α)−βraw. Upstream stores β = γ − αβraw and uses the exponent (β − γ)/α. The fork stores the positive asymptotic slope β = αβraw − γ and uses −(β + γ)/α (src/profiles_y.jl:65 at the tag). File lines are those of the tag. At the current HEAD 1660e08 of the cluster branch the file is unchanged, since only test/runtests.jl differs.

Numerical effect in this study: none. Every sky was painted with θ = B12, where the parametrised profile equals upstream's hard-coded one, and all ten skies used the same pinned engine. Differences between seeds and masks therefore cannot come from XGPaint. The edit matters for the training set, where θ varies row by row.

Not an XGPaint edit: the 4R200c sphere and the per-seed halo-count check (project side)

Upstream XGPaint integrates the gNFW profile along the whole line of sight (_nfw_profile_los_quadrature, out to 105 R200c) and only cuts the painted disc. The chord-limited 4R200c sphere is a project-side wrapper. ChordMeanProfile subtypes XGPaint.AbstractGNFW and extends two XGPaint methods by multiple dispatch. The XGPaint source itself is untouched:

"""Exact chord factor 2 L_max at angle theta given theta_max = X theta200c."""
@inline function chord_factor(theta, theta_max, X)
    ratio = theta / theta_max
    ratio >= 1 && return 0.0
    return 2X * sqrt(1 - ratio^2)
end

# XGPaint's profile_grid calls prepare_profile_slice once per (M, z) and
# evaluate_profile_slice per theta; both are extended for this type.
function XGPaint.prepare_profile_slice(m::ChordMeanProfile, mass, z)
    inner = m.inner
    Mu = mass * XGPaint.M_sun
    par = XGPaint.get_params(inner, Mu, z)
    θ200 = theta_r200c(inner, mass, z)
    xc, α, β, γ = Float64(par.xc), Float64(par.α), Float64(par.β), Float64(par.γ)
    A = projection_amplitude(inner, mass, z, θ200, xc, α, β, γ)
    return (; θ200, xc, α, β, γ, A, X=m.sphere)
end

@inline function XGPaint.evaluate_profile_slice(::ChordMeanProfile, p, theta, mass, z)
    x = Float64(theta) / p.θ200
    return p.A * chord_mean(x, p.xc, p.α, p.β, p.γ, p.X)
end

The cached quantity is the chord mean g = ysphere/(2L), which stays positive and smooth up to the sphere edge, so XGPaint's log interpolator works without modification. The painter multiplies g by the exact chord factor 2X√(1 − (θ/θmax)2) for each pixel (engine/balanced_painter.jl:30). It uses XGPaint only for ring geometry (get_relevant_rings and get_ring_disc_ranges, balanced_painter.jl:20-22).

The second project-side change is specific to this study. The frozen engine stops unless the catalogue yields exactly the training count, @assert count == 85224251 (tSZ_64k_flat_prior_SO_baseline_deproj0/engine/engine.jl:73). Other lightcones legitimately contain different halos, so paint_seed.jl:218-222 applies the exact count only to seed 100 and only bounds the count for the other seeds (see the excerpt above). Effect on numbers: none, since it only gates which runs may proceed. The seed-100 repaint still passed the exact count and reproduced the saved spectra.

4.3 First look: 19 observations at one Battaglia point (2 October)

The first experiment (SBI_analysis/tsz_seed_mask_variation_20261002) had two arms that share the baseline. The simulation arm painted lightcones 100-118 and observed each with the training mask. The mask arm observed lightcone 100 through masks 12345-12354. This gives 19 distinct observations, each passed through the frozen original Merlin ensemble with 90,000 posterior draws. The primary model is the unbounded one, a flow in linear parameter coordinates that can place mass outside the training box. The bounded companion uses a logit transform and stays inside the box.

The spectra themselves change little. Figure 4.2 shows that the masked clean spectra of other lightcones differ from the training sky by tens of per cent at ℓ < 500, where few modes and a few massive clusters dominate, and by about a per cent at ℓ > 2000. The robustness report quantifies the relative scatter below ℓ ≈ 580 as 10.7% across lightcones and 15.0% across masks, falling below 0.7% above ℓ ≈ 2980 (SBI_analysis/tsz_robustness_methods_20261004/final_results/INTERPRETATION.md).

The posteriors move far more than the spectra. The baseline gives a P0 median of 12.10 (68% interval 5.64-26.48). The nine other lightcones give medians of 45.50-53.66, and the nine other masks give 23.70-45.92 (final_results/parameter_comparison.csv). The study expresses shifts in units of the baseline half-width h = (q84 − q16)/2, the half-width of the baseline 68% interval. This is a fixed yardstick, not a Gaussian significance.

4.4 The 10 × 10 robustness experiment (4-5 October)

SBI_analysis/tsz_robustness_methods_20261004 completed the grid of Figure 4.1. Each new lightcone was painted once (nine painting jobs) and all ten masks were applied to that painting. Together with the inference, the matched Merlin controls and the final comparison, the study used twelve idark jobs. The grid served two purposes. It measured the sensitivity of the original model, and it tested 23 alternative inference recipes against it (4.7). Three summary numbers are used throughout. For case c and parameter i, let mci be the posterior median, wci the 68% width, θi* the truth and hi the half-width of the original model on the training sky:

T = √⟨[(mci − θi*) / hi]2⟩c,i   (truth displacement)
A = √⟨[(mci − m̄i) / hi]2⟩c,i   (agreement between skies)
G = exp ⟨ln(wci / wcioriginal)⟩c,i   (geometric-mean width ratio)

The denominator hi is fixed. A method cannot improve T or A by making its posteriors wider, and the width cost G is reported separately, compared case by case with the original model on the same sky. The joint report calls T "R" on its pages 10 and 17; the two are the same quantity. Beware that final_results/INTERPRETATION.md uses the letter R for the width ratio G instead.

def metrics(evaluation, reference, ids):
    """Use a fixed reference scale: broadening a candidate cannot hide its truth displacement."""
    med = np.array(evaluation['median'])[ids]
    width = np.array(evaluation['width68'])[ids]
    scale = np.array(reference['width68'])[0] / 2
    delta = (med - THETA) / scale
    variance = np.mean(((med - med.mean(0)) / scale)**2, axis=0)
    per_parameter_cost = np.exp(np.mean(np.log(width / np.array(reference['width68'])[ids]), axis=0))
    return dict(truth_rms=float(np.sqrt(np.mean(delta**2))),
                agreement_rms=float(np.sqrt(np.mean(variance))),
                width_cost=float(np.exp(np.mean(np.log(per_parameter_cost)))),
                worst_parameter_width_cost=float(np.max(per_parameter_cost)),

Line 44 is the fixed yardstick: half the original model's 68% width on the first case, the training sky. Line 47 takes the width ratio per parameter as a geometric mean over cases, and line 50 averages these over parameters. Line 51 records the worst parameter, because a 25% average cap can hide a 60-80% increase for one parameter, as 4.7 shows.

The heat map makes the central fact plain. The training sky is the odd one out. Nothing is wrong with the other 99 observations; they are simply not the sky the network was conditioned on. On the held-out cells the original model has T = 1.746 (lightcones), 1.419 (masks) and 1.660 (both new), with A = 0.157, 0.459 and 0.342. The masks give the larger disagreement between cells but a smaller common displacement.

Simulation, mask and interaction. Because the grid is complete, the variance of the medians can be split exactly in the manner of a two-way analysis of variance. With gsm the normalised median in cell (s, m), g̅ the grand mean, as = ⟨gsm⟩m − g̅ the lightcone effect and bm = ⟨gsm⟩s − g̅ the mask effect:

⟨(gsm − g̅)2⟩ = ⟨as2⟩ + ⟨bm2⟩ + ⟨(gsm − g̅ − as − bm)2⟩

This is implemented in SBI_analysis/tsz_robustness_methods_20261004/report.py:89-100 and asserted to sum exactly. For the original model the fractions are 26.3% lightcone, 31.8% mask and 41.9% interaction. The largest share is thus neither factor alone: the response to a new lightcone depends on the mask through which it is seen. That is what one expects if the network responds non-linearly to the fine spectral pattern, which depends on both. Because the masks overlap, these fractions are descriptive, not variance components of independent factors.

4.5 Offset diagnosis: why independent skies shift the same way (5 October)

Two observations needed explaining. First, nine statistically independent lightcones give P0 = 49.89 ± 2.32 and β = 13.01 ± 0.21, where ± is the scatter of the medians across skies and not a posterior uncertainty. The training sky gives P0 = 12.15 and β = 9.70, against a truth of 18.1 and 4.35 (SBI_analysis/tsz_seed_offset_diagnosis_20261005/seed_offset_summary.csv). Second, the spectra differ by only a few per cent. Compared with seed 100, the other lightcones average −6.14% at ℓ = 80-579, −0.063% at 580-2979 and +0.742% at 2980-7979. Lightcone 106 has the opposite low-ℓ sign (+15.8%) yet still gives P0 = 49.88 (diagnostics.json, spectral_blocks; parameter_comparison.csv). A uniform amplitude change cannot explain this. Clean tSZ power scales as P02, so tripling P0 would multiply the power by about nine.

Why a common direction is expected. Write the clean spectrum of lightcone i as an ensemble mean plus a sky fluctuation εi. Every comparison subtracts the same reference:

Δi = εi − ε100,    E[Δi | ε100] = −ε100,    Cov(Δi, Δj) = Cov(ε100) for i ≠ j (independent skies, equal covariance)

All nine differences contain the negative of seed 100's particular fluctuation. Their signs are therefore not nine independent tests of a parameter bias. If the network maps the training-sky fingerprint onto its learned θ → D relation, then removing that fingerprint, which every other sky does, pushes all nine posteriors the same way. The diagnosis tested this mechanism by constructing counterfactual inputs.

Counterfactual inputs. Starting from the saved training-sky observation D0, the diagnosis adds controlled pieces of the observed change and passes the result through the unchanged original network, with its whitening, PCA, asinh and learned head. The pieces are as follows. Actual is the real observation of another sky. Clean adds only the clean-signal change δDss. Cross adds only the change in the corrected signal-noise term (at a fixed mask the noise-noise term is identical and cancels). Reverse subtracts the clean change. Smooth adds a Gaussian-smoothed version of the fractional clean change. Fine adds the residual that the smoothing removes. Scale blocks add the clean change in one ℓ range only. The Gaussian kernel has standard deviation σℓ in multipole (σℓ = 5, 15, 50, 100), with four-sigma truncation and nearest-value edges.

Dsmooth = D0 + D100ss · Gσ[δD̄ss / D100ss],    Dfine = D0 + δD̄ss − D100ss · Gσ[δD̄ss / D100ss]

Here δD̄ss is the mean clean change of the nine other lightcones at mask 12345, and Gσ is the Gaussian filter in ℓ. Smooth and fine add up exactly to the clean change. These hybrids are not physical mock skies. They isolate the estimator's response to one component while leaving everything else fixed.

What was ruled out.

  • A painting or preprocessing difference. All lightcones share the h conversion, redshift selection, B12 vector, 4R200c sphere, beam, map resolutions and noise-map hashes (asserted at diagnose.py:99-102). The 1012 M⊙ mass cut is not in that assertion, but it is recorded identically in all ten painting contracts (diagnostics.json, contracts). The clean band powers reconstruct to 8.9 × 10−16, and the original inputs reconstruct exactly for all ten lightcones at the training mask.
  • BatchNorm or a single bad network. Batch-normalisation (BatchNorm) layers rescale their inputs with batch statistics during training; if they were left in training mode, a posterior would depend on which other inputs share its batch. All modules are in evaluation mode. Evaluating seed 100 in a different batch changes its log density by at most 1.91 × 10−6. All three ensemble members shift upwards.
  • Prior extrapolation of the unbounded flow. The bounded model shows the same pattern: P0 = 52.16 for the mean new observation, 11.67 for the smooth control and 44.50 for the fine residual (both at σℓ = 50; counterfactual_posteriors.csv, model original_bounded).
  • Inputs outside the training distribution. In the standardised network inputs, the nearest-neighbour distance from each new observation to the training pool lies at the 22nd-31st percentile of the same distance for 600 reserved rows (diagnostics.json, support.grid_percentile; the reports round this to 22nd-30th). They lie inside the global support. The mismatch is conditional: the broad prior contains spectra that resemble the new sky at the wrong θ.
  • A pure amplitude change. Adding +0.742% of clean power, the high-ℓ mean change, moves P0 only from 12.23 to 12.43.
  • Noise fluctuation. The 21 earlier B12 noise draws on the training sky gave P0 medians of 9.3-23.6, far below the common value of about 50. Twenty of those draws entered the whitening covariance, so they are a conditional check, not an independent calibration.

Why P0, β and the exponents move together. At fixed mass, redshift and radius, with u = x/xc,

d ln Pe = d ln P0 + (0.3 + βu/(1 + u)) d ln xc − ln(1 + u) dβ

Raising P0 raises the pressure, raising β lowers it at finite radius, and raising xc raises it again. These directions can compensate one another, and the six exponents add further freedom by shifting which masses and redshifts dominate each angular scale. A high P0 with a high β and changed α values therefore does not imply proportionally more tSZ power. The shift is a coordinated move of all nine coordinates, not a slide along one degeneracy of the training-sky posterior. On the training sky the posterior correlations are P0-β −0.34 and P0-xc −0.65, yet the medians of the other skies move P0, β and xc all upwards, together with αm,P0, αm,β and αz,β, while αm,xc, αz,P0 and αz,xc move down (seed_offset_summary.csv). The posteriors on the new skies also have a somewhat different shape: averaged over the nine lightcones at the training mask, xc-β stays at 0.82 (0.81 on the training sky), but P0-xc weakens to −0.39 and P0-β to −0.04 (posterior_correlations.json, 10,000 joint draws per sky; REPORT.md quotes the first two). The local derivative matrix of the spectrum with respect to θ is badly conditioned: its prior-scaled singular-value ratio is about 1.2 × 10−5 (diagnostics.json, linear_scores), so some parameter combinations barely change the spectrum. A small, structured spectral change can therefore move the nine-dimensional solution a long way along these nearly flat directions. The REPORT warns that the linear inverse is too ill-conditioned to predict the nonlinear shifts quantitatively.

The training-sky posterior is itself displaced in its marginal medians (P0 12.2 and β 9.7 against 18.1 and 4.35), yet the truth sits in a high-density region of the joint posterior. Its joint highest-posterior-density (HPD) level, the posterior mass at higher density than the density at the truth (defined fully in 4.7), is 0.072, so it lies on the boundary of the 7.2% highest-density region (counterfactual_posteriors.csv, baseline row, 9,000 draws; the 6,000-draw joint test of 4.7 gives 0.073). This displacement is present on the training sky itself, so it is not a seed or mask effect. The joint report lists nonlinear degeneracy (the xc-β correlation is +0.81), prior volume and finite density-estimator error as possible causes and states that their relative contributions have not been separated (page 26). Restoring agreement with the training sky (the smooth controls) restores this conditional answer, including its marginal offsets. The smooth control is a counterfactual and cannot serve as a bias correction: it needs the clean training-sky spectrum at the known B12 truth as its reference, which real data do not provide.

The joint report (SBI_analysis/tsz_joint_report_20261005, 36 pages) added one new inference. Section 4.5 showed that adding only the smooth part of the sky change leaves the posterior medians close to the training-sky answer. The Pearson test asks whether that smooth part also leaves the shape of the posterior, its correlation structure, unchanged. If it did not, the smooth/fine split of 4.5 would be an incomplete description of the mechanism. The Pearson matrix is then np.corrcoef(draws, rowvar=False) over all 90,000 physical-parameter draws without prior clipping (baseline_pearson.py:55). It measures sampling precision only, not training uncertainty.

Pearson r between posterior parameters a and b is rab = Cov(θa, θb)/(σaσb), computed over the draws of one posterior. It describes the orientation of the posterior cloud. It is a different quantity from the correlation between true parameters and posterior means across many test cases, used in the 6 October supplement and Section 3. That correlation cannot be measured on this grid, because the truth is the same in every sky.

4.7 Quantified impact and the mitigations tried

The diagnosis shows that the original model's answer depends on information that is specific to its training sky. The robustness study tested whether a different compression could suppress that information without discarding too much real pressure information. All 23 new recipes start from 50-multipole bands. The 7,900 values of Dℓ are averaged in contiguous blocks of 50 (80-129, 130-179, …, 7930-7979), giving 158 band powers. Each multipole is weighted by 2ℓ + 1, the number of its harmonic modes on a full sky (SBI_analysis/tsz_robustness_methods_20261004/common.py:48-54). The covariance is transformed consistently, Cband = A C AT. Band50 is not Gaussian smoothing with σℓ = 50, which is the Pearson control of 4.6, and it is not an average over 50 simulations. Its construction and training are part of Section 5. Sixteen of the recipes compress the bands with MOPED, a linear compression to nine numbers built from the local derivatives of the spectrum (defined below the table); the other seven retrain the Merlin network on the bands.

One loop builds every recipe from the same 158 bands. Cuts keep complete bands only, with boundaries rounded inwards (line 154). The sky-nuisance recipes add the empirical discovery-sky covariance S of the clean residuals (lines 158-159). The taper (lines 160-162) scales the standard deviation of each band by s = √(1 + 9 min(1, max(0, (ℓc − 4000)/3980))2), where ℓc is the band centre. Element (i, j) of the covariance is multiplied by sisj, so the band variance is unchanged below ℓ = 4000 and is inflated up to about ten times (standard deviation up to about √10) at the top of the range. The Merlin branch (lines 164-168) keeps the original noise-only convention and whitens the 158 bands directly, with no PCA step. Band50 Merlin therefore differs from the original in the banding, the banded whitening, the refitted asinh transform and the retraining. The prior, the 61,440-row pool, the three seeds and the head/flow architecture are the same. The experiment tests the whole recipe; it does not isolate the averaging alone.

Selection rule. Fixed in advance and evaluated on the 30 discovery skies only; no held-out sky was used for tuning or ranking. The rule picks the smallest √(T2 + A2) among the recipes that improve both T and A over the original, pass a broad marginal and joint calibration screen on reserved prior rows, and keep the geometric-mean width ratio at G ≤ 1.25. The calibration screen uses the 1,500 even-indexed reserved rows. Its marginal part checks the fraction of rows whose true parameter falls inside the 68% and 95% marginal intervals (coverage). Its joint part uses random-reference distance ranks, the test of Lemos et al. 2023 (TARP, arXiv:2302.03026). For each row it computes the fraction of posterior draws that lie closer to a random reference point than the truth does. For a calibrated posterior this fraction is uniform over rows, so the share of rows with a fraction below 0.68 (0.95) should be 68% (95%). Both parts must be within 0.10 (68% level) and 0.06 (95% level) of nominal, the marginal part for every one of the nine parameters (selection.json, calibration_screen; SBI_analysis/tsz_robustness_methods_20261004/final_report.py:44-46). The 25% cap was chosen by the user. Under it, retrained band50 Merlin over the full ℓ = 80-7979 range won in all three arms (final_results/report/selection.json). No recipe qualified within a 10% budget. With a 50% budget, sky-weighted MOPED wins: the combined-sky covariance with λ = 1 in the lightcone and combined arms, and its weak λ = 0.1 version in the mask arm (selection.json, width_budget_winners). Their full-set width costs are about 37-40% (INTERPRETATION.md); on the held-out skies the λ = 1 variant costs 35-39% (joint report page 20).

Joint truth containment. Marginal medians can be displaced while the truth still lies in a high-density part of the joint posterior. The joint test computes the posterior mass at density higher than the density at the truth (the HPD level). A level below 0.68 means the truth is inside the 68% highest-posterior-density region. Densities must be compared in physical coordinates. For the bounded flow, which lives in logit space u, both the draws and the truth need the Jacobian of the transform θ(u). The joint report lists applying the Jacobian to both sample and truth densities among the source fixes made during the study (page 35) and calls it the density-coordinate fix (page 21):

        ens=mc.ensemble(folder,variant,threads)
        torch.manual_seed(7103)
        u=ens.sample_u(x,6000,rng=np.random.default_rng(7103))
        # Compare densities in the SAME physical parameter coordinates, not logit density.
        log_samples=ens.log_prob_u(u,x)-log_jacobian_theta_per_u(u,ens.low,ens.high)
        truth=to_u(np.broadcast_to(THETA,(len(x),9)),ens.low,ens.high)
        log_truth=ens.log_prob_u(truth[None],x)[0]-log_jacobian_theta_per_u(truth,ens.low,ens.high)
        hpd=np.mean(log_samples>log_truth,axis=0)

Lines 46 and 48 subtract the same log-Jacobian from the draws and from the truth, so the comparison is between densities in θ. Line 49 is the HPD level of the truth for each sky. Each sky uses 6,000 fresh draws with seed 7103, giving a rank MCSE of √(h(1 − h)/6000).

The diagnosis confirmed that band50 does not react to the fingerprint. Its P0 median is 32.65 for the training-sky observation and 32.78 for the mean new observation. The ten inputs of Figure 4.7 give 32.65-33.24, and all 104 diagnosis inputs, including the individual real skies, give 32.12-34.20 (counterfactual_posteriors.csv, model band50_linear). Most of the fine pattern that drove the original model varies from multipole to multipole and is averaged out inside each 50-multipole band. Band50 also differs from the original in its whitening and training, so this insensitivity cannot be credited to the averaging alone.

The ℓ ≥ 580 cut. Because the sky variance of the spectrum is concentrated below ℓ ≈ 580, a matched band50 Merlin was also trained without the ten lowest bands. It keeps 148 of 158 bands (ℓ = 580-7979, 7,400 of 7,900 multipoles). This removes the largest angular scales, roughly π/580 ≈ 19 arcmin and above, not the small ones.

The cost over the whole prior. The 18-29% costs above (band50 17.9-24.7%, ℓ ≥ 580 22.5-28.7%) were measured at one point, θ = B12, on the grid skies. The 6 October validation (SBI_analysis/tsz_truth_mean_validation_20261006) evaluated the same frozen models on 1,500 reserved rows spread over the whole prior. These rows still use the training lightcone and mask, but θ and the noise vary. The width cost there is larger: +45.64% for band50 unbounded, +46.41% for band50 bounded and +56.51% for the ℓ ≥ 580 control. The correlation between truth and posterior mean drops for every parameter: P0 0.733 → 0.537 → 0.508, xc 0.790 → 0.694 → 0.645, β 0.696 → 0.478 → 0.390 (original → band50 → ℓ ≥ 580; README of that directory). Marginal 68% coverage stays acceptable, at 63.2-66.8% for band50 unbounded and 68.3-73.0% bounded. A further measure is the mean log density at the truth, log q(θtrue | x) in physical parameter coordinates averaged over the reserved rows; it rewards posteriors that are both narrow and correct. In the 5 October final test on the same 1,500 rows it falls from −3.013 ± 0.136 (original) to −5.481 ± 0.097 (band50, unbounded), where ± is the standard error over rows (joint report, page 21). This is another measure of the information lost.

Why do the two costs differ? The validation README attributes the difference to the different observations and pressure parameters over which the widths are averaged. One plausible reading, not tested separately, is that many prior rows are bright and well measured, so the within-band structure carries real pressure information that band50 averages away, whereas at B12 the original posterior is already wide in the affected directions. Both numbers include the effect of retraining and changed preprocessing, not only the averaging itself. No sweep of band widths has been made: 50 was a practical choice, and widths of 25 or 100 would require refitted transforms and retrained networks.

4.8 What the seed/mask study establishes, and what is open

The original 64k Merlin posterior is conditional on lightcone 100 and mask 12345. On other skies with the identical B12 pressure law it shifts P0 from about 12 to about 50, and the B12 truth falls outside the 95% joint region on almost every other sky. The cause is the fine multipole fingerprint of the clean training-sky spectrum, which the network treats as signal. Retrained band50 Merlin removes 68-79% of the disagreement between skies and puts the truth inside the joint 68% region on all 100 skies. It costs about 18-25% wider intervals at B12 and about 46% averaged over the prior. The ℓ ≥ 580 cut adds cost without a combined benefit.
  • Agreement, truth and calibration are separate statements. Band50 varies far less between observations (agreement). It recovers B12 in joint density but not in every marginal median (truth). Its reserved-row marginal 68% coverage is close to nominal (63.2-66.8% in the 6 October validation with 9,000 draws per row; 63.3-66.9% in the 5 October final test, INTERPRETATION.md), but only on the fixed training sky (calibration). None of the tests certifies inference on unseen halo realisations across the prior.
  • The grid is conditional. One noise pair, overlapping masks and a single θ. The scatter measures sensitivity, not cosmic-variance coverage. Ten skies at one pressure point cannot define a nuisance covariance that is valid across the nine-dimensional prior.
  • The principled remedy has not been run. The remedy is to train with the lightcone and mask drawn independently of θ for each row, for example from the existing pool of ten lightcones and many mask seeds. This needs the training rows to be repainted, but no new N-body simulations. Entire lightcones and masks would be held out for validation, at several pressure points. A nuisance-hardened compression (Alsing & Wandelt 2019, arXiv:1903.01473) or Ceff = Cnoise + Csky weighting is a possible interim step. It would have to enter the simulator and training consistently.
  • XGPaint is not implicated. Every sky used the same pinned fork tag at θ = B12. The only code changes were project-side painting orchestration (the per-seed count check), not physics.
Open issues. (1) The training-sky marginal offsets (β ≈ 9.7 against 4.35) remain, and they are not a seed effect. (2) The bandwidth (25, 50 or 100) is not optimised. (3) The augmentation recipes approximate sky variation with B12 clean-spectrum ratios and keep the SO residual unchanged. They were not validated at other θ. (4) The counterfactual and Pearson controls are diagnostics and must not be used to correct real data.
