5. Merlin and 50-multipole bands

This section takes the compression that gave the best marginal constraints in the 64k tSZ simulation-based inference (SBI) analysis, called Merlin, apart step by step: from the 7,900 measured multipoles, through noise whitening, principal components, an asinh rescaling and a learned compression head, to the posterior. It then explains the 50-multipole-band variant ("band50") that was built so that the inference does not jump when the halo realisation or the sky mask changes. The short version: Merlin beats whitened PCA, MOPED and 40 band powers on point accuracy (mean Pearson r 0.729 against 0.590 for 40 bands, beam-consistent noise), but the evidence indicates that much of that lead comes from structure finer than 50 multipoles that belongs to the one HalfDome sky the network was trained on. Averaging it away (band50) removes most of the sky sensitivity, costs about 46 % in posterior width over the prior and brings the accuracy back to the 40-band level.

5.1 What "Merlin" means in this project

Merlin is the name of a published data-compression method, not a person or a machine. The project's implementation, merlin_compression.py, cites Wernersson, Franco-Abellan and Cañas-Herrera (2026), arXiv:2609.24946, Section 3.3, and two modules of their code, merlin/preprocessing.py and merlin/network.py. Neither the paper nor the original code is stored on this machine (a file-system search found only the project's own files), so this section describes the re-implementation exactly as the code and its docstrings define it. Where the text says what the original does, the statement comes from the docstring of merlin_compression.py (lines 1 to 33; the noise redraw is stated on line 31).

The idea in one sentence: whiten the full-resolution spectrum with a noise covariance, project it onto the leading principal components of the whitened noise-free training spectra, and let a neural network learn two summary numbers per parameter from these components, trained together with the posterior estimator. The method sits between two simpler compressions that were already in the analysis. 40 bins averages the 7,900 multipoles into 40 band powers of width 200 (the last one 100 wide), each multipole weighted by its 2ℓ + 1 modes, and feeds them to the posterior network. MOPED (Massively Optimised Parameter Estimation and Data compression, Heavens, Jimenez and Lahav 2000, astro-ph/9911102) projects the data onto nine fixed directions, one per parameter, built from the derivatives of the mean spectrum at the Battaglia et al. (2012) point (B12) and a noise covariance; it is optimal only for a Gaussian likelihood near that point. Whitened PCA (principal component analysis) is Merlin without the learned head: the same components go straight into the posterior network.

All variants end in the same density estimator, so that only the compression differs. The estimator is neural posterior estimation (NPE): a conditional normalising flow q(θ | x) is trained on pairs (θ, x) drawn from the prior and the simulator to maximise the mean log density of the true parameters, which makes q approach the posterior for every x at once. The flow is a masked autoregressive flow (MAF) from the sbi package with 5 transforms of 128 hidden units, applied to transformed parameters u (section 5.8).

5.2 The data vector: 7,900 unbinned multipoles with beam-consistent noise

Each simulation row is a parameter vector θ of the nine generalised NFW (gNFW, the generalised Navarro-Frenk-White pressure profile) parameters: the pressure amplitude P0, the core scale xc and the outer slope β, each with a mass slope αm and a redshift slope αz. They are drawn from a flat prior box (P0 1 to 60, xc 0.025 to 4, β 2.8 to 16, αmP0 −0.6 to 1.5, αmxc −1 to 0.4, αmβ −0.2 to 0.4, αzP0 −6 to 0.5, αzxc −1.5 to 3, αzβ −0.5 to 2; fisher_forecast_b12.py lines 63 to 64). The frozen engine paints a Compton-y map on the HalfDome lightcone, smooths it with a 2′ beam, applies an apodised cap mask with fsky = 0.4, adds two independent Simons Observatory (SO) noise splits and stores the signed cross spectrum of the two splits. Merlin works on

Dℓ = ℓ(ℓ+1) Cℓ / 2π,   ℓ = 80, 81, …, 7979   (7,900 numbers per simulation)

with no binning. The collaborator's file of these spectra (65,536 rows × 7,900 multipoles, float64, 4.1 GB) was streamed in chunks of 2,048 rows. Every pass checks that averaging the per-multipole spectra into the 40 analysis bands reproduces both the collaborator's and our own 40-band files to a relative 10−9, and that the raw MOPED projections agree (merlin_unbinned_prepare.py lines 194 to 212). Of the 65,536 design rows, 64,440 were analysed; a fixed permutation sets aside 3,000 test rows, and the remaining 61,440 form the training pool, whose first N rows are the nested training sets of the size series.

From 2026-10-02 on, the Merlin inputs use beam-consistent spectra. The production engine beamed the signal but added the beam-deconvolved SO noise without the beam, which overstates the noise × noise term by 1/Bℓ2 and the signal × noise term by 1/Bℓ. Instead of repainting, the noise cross spectrum Cnn of every row was regenerated from its stored seeds, and each noise term was given the beam:

Cbeamℓ = Cssℓ + Bℓ (Cnoisyℓ − Cssℓ − Cnnℓ) + Bℓ2 Cnnℓ

(merlin_beam_prepare.py lines 71 to 73), with Css the noise-free (beamed, masked) signal spectrum, Cnoisy the stored production cross spectrum of the two splits, Cnn the cross spectrum of the two noise splits alone and Bℓ the transfer function of the 2′ beam. Two checks guard the inputs (results_merlin_unbinned_beam/inputs/prepare_whitening.json, "checks"; code in merlin_beam_prepare.py lines 150 to 166): the uploaded per-multipole production and noise-free spectra rebin to the stored 40-band values with a largest difference of 2.6×10−15 (noisy) and 1.7×10−15 (clean) of each row's largest band power, and the rebuilt beam-consistent spectra equal the staged beam-fix run's own 40-band and per-multipole spectra exactly ("beam40" and "beam_unbinned" both 0). The beam fix itself is documented on the explainer page (its section 3, results_doc/index.html); here it only defines the input, and section 5.9 shows that it changed almost nothing in the SBI metrics.

5.3 Step 1: whitening, from 20 draws to a 420-draw lag model

Whitening transforms the spectrum so that its noise has unit variance and no correlation between multipoles: with a noise covariance C = LLT (L its lower-triangular Cholesky factor), x̃ = L−1x. Two features of the raw spectrum make this necessary. The noise standard deviation per multipole spans more than a factor 30 (6.4×10−15 to 2.3×10−13 in Dℓ units for the production noise, prepare.json of results_merlin_unbinned), so an unweighted PCA would be dominated by the noisiest multipoles. And the apodised mask couples neighbouring multipoles: in the 420-draw covariance model below, the noise correlation between ℓ and ℓ+1 is 0.551. After whitening, a distance in the PCA step is a distance in noise units. Three covariances were used, in this order.

(a) The 20 B12 noise draws, shrunk with OAS

The first Merlin inputs (2026-09-29 to 2026-10-01) used the 21 stored SO noise draws of the B12 mock, leaving out the draw of the B12 observation itself (noise seeds 39001001 and 39001002) so that the observation's noise is not part of its own whitening. With 20 draws and 7,900 multipoles the sample covariance is singular, so it is regularised with Oracle Approximating Shrinkage (OAS, Chen et al. 2010): the sample matrix S is shrunk towards a scaled identity with a weight ρ computed from the data. The project applies OAS to the correlation matrix of the standardised draws, renormalises it to a unit diagonal and multiplies back the measured variances, so the variances are kept as measured. Written out (the explicit form is in fisher_forecast_b12.py lines 219 to 224):

Sz = ZTZ / n (Z the n × p matrix of standardised draws),   R̂ = (1 − ρ) Sz + ρ μ I,   μ = tr Sz / p,   ρ = min{ (α + μ2) / [(n + 1)(α − μ2/p)], 1 },   α = mean of the squared entries of Sz
R̃ℓℓ′ = R̂ℓℓ′ / (R̂ℓℓ R̂ℓ′ℓ′)1/2 (unit diagonal),   C = D R̃ D,   D = diag(σℓ),   n = 20 draws, p = 7,900 multipoles
def whitener():
    """Noise covariance of the unbinned spectra and its Cholesky factor.

    so_nine_fisher.conditional_covariance, computed through sklearn's _oas: sklearn.covariance.oas is the same
    estimate but also forms the pseudo-inverse of the 7,900 x 7,900 matrix, which takes hours and is not needed.
    Uses the 20 draws of mocks/pass1_B12/B12 other than B12_FITTED.
    Returns (L, info): L (7900, 7900) lower triangular with L L^T = C, in D_ell units; info the dict stored as
    'covariance' in clean.json (draws, oas_correlation_shrinkage, diag_sd_range = [min, max] of sqrt(diag C))."""
    from sklearn.covariance._shrunk_covariance import _oas
    draws = _b12_draws([p for p in sorted(B12_MOCKS.glob('noisy_cross_*.npy')) if p != B12_FITTED])
    std = draws.std(axis=0, ddof=1)
    assert np.all(std > 0), 'zero ensemble variance'
    # shrink the correlation of the standardised draws, not the covariance: the variances are kept as measured
    standardised = (draws - draws.mean(axis=0)) / std
    correlation, shrinkage = _oas(standardised, assume_centered=True)
    # OAS normalises by n, the standardisation by n - 1: rescale to a unit diagonal
    correlation /= np.sqrt(np.outer(np.diag(correlation), np.diag(correlation)))
    C = correlation * np.outer(std, std)
    L = np.linalg.cholesky(C)
    diag_sd = np.sqrt(C.diagonal())
    info = dict(draws=len(draws), oas_correlation_shrinkage=shrinkage,
                diag_sd_range=[float(diag_sd.min()), float(diag_sd.max())])
    return L, info

The estimator works on the correlation of the standardised draws, so the measured per-multipole variances (std) survive the shrinkage exactly; the renormalisation line corrects the n versus n − 1 convention of sklearn's private _oas. With 20 draws the result is ρ = 0.9517 (oas_correlation_shrinkage in prepare.json): the correlation matrix is 95 % identity, so this whitening is in practice a variance-only, diagonal one.

Two weaknesses follow. The 20 draws cannot resolve the mask correlation (their shrunk correlation between ℓ and ℓ+1 is near zero, Figure 5.1b), and because they are draws of the B12 sky the covariance contains the B12 signal × noise term, so it depends on a fiducial model.

(c) The 420-draw lag covariance with a white floor

For the Fisher forecast (2026-10-02, idark root ~/tsz64k_fisher_b12_20261002), 400 further B12 noise realisations of the noise on the B12 sky were painted (split-seed pairs 950001/950002 to 950799/950800, idark_fisher/make_tasks.py) and their Cnn regenerated, so the B12 sky has 420 beam-consistent noise residuals r = x − xclean besides the observation. Twenty draws cannot fill a 7,900 × 7,900 matrix, but 420 can estimate a model with few parameters: a smooth variance and a correlation that depends only on the lag ℓ′ − ℓ.

C = diag(s) R diag(s),   sℓ2 = running mean of ⟨rℓ2⟩ over ℓ ± 25,   Rℓℓ′ = ρ|ℓ−ℓ′| (Toeplitz, lags < 64),   then Cℓℓ → Cℓℓ + 10−3 sℓ2
def welch_acf(z, lags):
    """Autocorrelation of the rows of z at lags 0..lags-1: Hann segments of length `lags` (half overlap, plus one
    segment ending at the last multipole), averaged over segments and rows. It is the Fourier series of the average
    windowed periodogram, a non-negative spectral density, so its Toeplitz matrices are positive semi-definite."""
    w = np.hanning(lags + 2)[1:-1]
    starts = sorted(set(range(0, z.shape[1] - lags + 1, lags // 2)) | {z.shape[1] - lags})
    acc = np.zeros(lags)
    norm = 0.0
    for s in starts:
        f = np.fft.rfft(z[:, s:s + lags] * w, n=2 * lags, axis=1)
        acc += np.fft.irfft(np.abs(f) ** 2, n=2 * lags, axis=1)[:, :lags].sum(0)
        norm += len(z) * np.sum(w ** 2)
    return acc / norm


def covariance_model(r, lags, half):
    """C = diag(s) R diag(s) from residuals r (n, 7900) of known zero mean. Returns C, s (7900,), rho (lags,)."""
    s = np.sqrt(running_mean(np.mean(r ** 2, 0), half))
    acf = welch_acf(r / s, lags)
    rho = acf / acf[0]
    column = np.zeros(P)
    column[:lags] = rho
    return toeplitz(column) * np.outer(s, s), s, rho

The lag correlation ρk is the Welch estimate: Hann-windowed (raised-cosine tapered) segments of 64 multipoles with half overlap, averaged periodograms, inverse FFT. Because it is the Fourier series of a non-negative spectral density, the Toeplitz matrix is positive semi-definite by construction, which a raw sample autocorrelation would not guarantee.

The model gives ρ1..5 = 0.551, 0.049, 0.032, 0.026, 0.005 (prepare.json of results_merlin_unbinned_beam_w420_asinh_p30). The spectral density of the lag correlation, S(ω) = 1 + 2Σkρkcos(kω), gives the noise variance (in units of the per-multipole variance) of a pattern that oscillates with frequency ω in ℓ, and the eigenvalues of a large Toeplitz matrix fill the range of S(ω). At the alternating frequency, S(π) = 1 + 2Σk(−1)kρk = 7.7×10−7; at ω = 0 it is S(0) = 2.41 (results_fisher_b12_beam/fisher_forecast_b12.json, "covariance"): a pattern that flips sign from one multipole to the next carries almost no noise, because the mask correlates neighbours positively. Whitening divides each direction by the square root of its noise, so this one poorly known direction would be amplified by about 1/√(7.7×10−7) ≈ 1,100. The white floor of 10−3 of the variance lifts the smallest eigenvalue of R to 0.00100 ("min_eigenvalue_R") and limits the amplification to about 32.

5.4 Step 2: principal components of the whitened noise-free spectra

PCA finds the orthogonal directions along which a set of vectors varies most. Here the vectors are the whitened noise-free spectra wj = L−1xjclean of the 61,440 training rows. The 4.1 GB file does not fit in memory, so the 7,900 × 7,900 covariance of the wj is accumulated chunk by chunk and diagonalised; its eigenvectors are the principal axes and the singular values of the centred data matrix are Sk = √(nλk). Following the original code, of the top 500 components those whose share of the summed singular values exceeds 0.001 % are kept, and every spectrum (noisy, clean, the B12 observation and its draws) is projected as s = (L−1x)V.

The first components describe the amplitude and broad shape of the spectrum, which change by orders of magnitude over the prior. A small, rapidly varying change of the spectrum with θ has almost no variance across the prior and falls below the cut. The Fisher forecast quantifies this: at B12, with beam-consistent noise, the geometric mean over the nine parameters of the marginal Fisher standard deviation is 0.15 prior widths if every multipole is used, 0.59 for Merlin's 86 PCA inputs and 1.66 for the 40 bands (Figure 5.11; results_fisher_b12_beam/fisher_forecast_b12.json, whose key still says "Merlin 75 PCA inputs" but checks n_pca = 86). Measured by these marginal widths, the PCA inputs constrain the parameters 2.8 times better than the 40 bands and 4 times worse than the full spectrum at this point.

5.5 Step 3: the asinh rescaling and its scale

The projections s still span many decades across the flat prior: B12 sits at the 29th percentile of tSZ power at ℓ ≈ 1980 and the prior covers more than ten decades (explainer page, section 1). A linear z-score (subtract the pool mean, divide by the pool standard deviation) is then set by the brightest rows and leaves most rows, B12 included, within about 10−3 standard deviations of each other. The first Merlin runs used such linear inputs. From 2026-10-01 every component is first passed through an inverse hyperbolic sine at the noise level:

xj = [ asinh((sj − mj) / cj) − z̄j ] / σz,j,   mj = pool median of sj,   cj = 1.4826 · median |rj − median rj|,   r = snoisy − sclean

asinh(y) ≈ y for |y| < 1 and ≈ sign(y) ln(2|y|) beyond, so the transform is linear within one noise unit of the centre and logarithmic across the decades of the prior. The scale cj is the noise in component j, measured robustly: 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation (MAD) equals the standard deviation for a Gaussian, and unlike the sample standard deviation it is not pulled up by the bright rows, whose noise is larger because of the signal × noise term. The parameters are fitted on the training pool and frozen; test rows and observations use the same values.

def fit_asinh(s_noisy, s_clean):
    """Parameters of the asinh input transform, fitted on the training pool.

    s_noisy, s_clean: (rows, n_pca) projections of the whitened noisy and noise-free training spectra.
    Returns a dict of (n_pca,) float64 arrays: s_center (pool median of s_noisy), s_scale (noise scale), and
    z_mean, z_sd (pool mean and sd of z = arcsinh((s_noisy - s_center) / s_scale)).
    """
    # noise scale: the sd of the noise part r = s_noisy - s_clean, from its median absolute deviation, which is not
    # pulled up by the bright rows (their sample variance is large)
    residual = s_noisy - s_clean
    scale = MAD_TO_SD * np.median(np.abs(residual - np.median(residual, axis=0)), axis=0)
    if not np.all(scale > 0):
        raise ValueError('zero noise scale in a PCA component')
    center = np.median(s_noisy, axis=0)
    # arcsinh(y) ~ y for |y| < 1 and ~ sign(y) log(2|y|) beyond: linear within a noise unit of the centre,
    # logarithmic across the decades of the prior
    z = np.arcsinh((s_noisy - center) / scale)
    return dict(s_center=center, s_scale=scale, z_mean=z.mean(0), z_sd=z.std(0))


def asinh_standardize(s, params):
    """The asinh inputs of projections s (rows, n_pca): (arcsinh((s - s_center) / s_scale) - z_mean) / z_sd.

    params: the dict of fit_asinh. Returns float64 of the shape of s, of pool mean 0 and sd 1 per component.
    """
    z = np.arcsinh((s - params['s_center']) / params['s_scale'])
    return (z - params['z_mean']) / params['z_sd']

The noise part r = snoisy − sclean exists only in simulations, which is why the scale is learned from the pool and stored. The scale ranges over components from 4.10 to 6.94 (production, 20-draw whitening), 3.27 to 6.20 (beam-consistent noise-only) and 2.23 to 4.77 (420-draw); if a row's noise had exactly the whitening covariance, its projected noise would have unit standard deviation in every component (whitened noise is white, V has orthonormal columns), so cj = 1. Values of 2 to 7 mean that the typical training row is noisier than the whitening covariance in these directions. Two effects can do this (an inference, not a separate test): the signal × noise term, which grows with the brightness of the sky (the pool median is about 14 × B12) and which the noise-only covariance does not contain at all and the 20-draw and 420-draw covariances contain only at B12 brightness; and, for the 20-draw whitening, the neighbour correlation that OAS shrank away, which by itself inflates the noise of a smooth direction by up to √S(0) ≈ 1.6 (S(0) = 2.41, results_fisher_b12_beam/fisher_forecast_b12.json, "covariance").

The effect was large. With the same patience of 30 epochs, test log p (the mean log posterior density of the true parameters over the 3,000 test rows, in natural-log units, nats; section 5.9) of the Merlin ensemble went from −5.01 (linear inputs, results_merlin_unbinned_p30) to −3.38 ± 0.06 (asinh, results_merlin_unbinned_asinh_p30), PCA from −8.73 to −4.67 and the B12 truth log p of Merlin from −8.70 to −4.12. The flat posteriors of the first runs were mostly an input-scaling problem.

The transform has a known weakness. It is centred on the pool median, which is a sky about 14 times brighter than B12 at ℓ ≈ 1980. A sky much fainter than B12 has projections close to zero, about 1,300 and 2,100 noise units below the centre in the first two components, deep in the logarithmic branch. There a tenfold change in a faint sky moves the input by a few thousandths of its unit scale (0.01 to 0.1 × B12) and by about 10−5 below 0.01 × B12 (explainer page, section 7). The ordering survives, because asinh is monotonic, but the differences become tiny, and on those rows Merlin and PCA stay close to the flat-prior log p of −12.9 (section 5.9). The 40-band asinh is centred on zero and does not have this problem. A zero-centred asinh for the PCA inputs has not been tried.

5.6 Step 4: the learned head and the flow

The standardised components enter the head, a fully connected network with two hidden layers of 2048 and 512 ReLU units and a linear output of 9 × 2 = 18 features followed by batch normalisation (BatchNorm1d keeps the features at unit scale for the flow). The 18 features are the condition of the MAF. Head and flow are one network: the head is passed to sbi as embedding_net, so the gradient of the posterior loss trains both. This is the main departure from the original, which trains the head with marginal ratio estimators.

The head makes Merlin a much larger network: 1,409,296 trainable parameters with 75 inputs, 1,425,680 with 83 and 1,431,824 with 86, against 231,770 and 236,890 for the PCA flow with 75 and 83 inputs (the parameters field of the training records *_seed1.json). What the head learns can be seen by correlating each of the 18 features with the nine parameters over the test rows (Figure 5.4). The features are not tied to single parameters: most mix several, and no correlation exceeds about 0.6. The head hands the flow a compact nonlinear summary and the flow separates the parameters.

The head on its own is not what helps. Feeding all 7,900 asinh-scaled multipoles to the same head ("raw", no whitening or PCA) gave the worst result of all compressions at 32,000 training rows: test log p −7.21 and mean r 0.542, against −4.60 and 0.709 for Merlin at the same size (results_corrected_inputs/summary.md). The whitening and PCA reduce the input to a basis in which a network of this size can learn.

5.7 Training recipe: AdamW, patience 30/10, three seeds

Each (variant, seed) is trained by merlin_compression.train_one. A seed-dependent permutation puts 20 % of the training pool into validation. AdamW (Adam with decoupled weight decay) minimises the mean −log q(u | x) over mini-batches of 256. PyTorch's ReduceLROnPlateau (default settings apart from the factor) divides the learning rate by 10 once the validation loss has failed to improve by a relative 10−4 for more than lr_patience epochs, that is at the 11th such epoch for lr_patience = 10 (the explainer page, section 10, quotes it as "×0.1 after 11 epochs"); training stops after patience epochs without an absolute improvement larger than 10−6 (MIN_GAIN); the best validation state is restored and saved.

    perm = torch.from_numpy(np.random.default_rng(seed).permutation(len(u)))

    val, fit = perm[:n_val], perm[n_val:]
    optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(net.parameters(), lr=SETTINGS['lr'])
    scheduler = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.ReduceLROnPlateau(optimizer, factor=SETTINGS['lr_factor'],

    best_loss = np.inf
    best_state = None
    epochs_without_gain = 0
    history = []
    start = time.monotonic()
    for epoch in range(SETTINGS['max_epochs']):
        net.train()
        shuffled = fit[torch.randperm(len(fit))]
        for i in range(0, len(shuffled), SETTINGS['batch']):
            batch = shuffled[i:i + SETTINGS['batch']]
            if len(batch) < 2:                       # BatchNorm1d needs two rows in train mode
                continue
            optimizer.zero_grad()
            loss = net.loss(u[batch], condition=x[batch]).mean()
            loss.backward()
            optimizer.step()
        net.eval()
        with torch.no_grad():
            chunks = [net.loss(u[val[i:i + VAL_CHUNK]], condition=x[val[i:i + VAL_CHUNK]])
                      for i in range(0, len(val), VAL_CHUNK)]
            val_loss = float(torch.cat(chunks).mean())
        scheduler.step(val_loss)
        history.append(dict(epoch=epoch + 1, val_loss=val_loss, lr=optimizer.param_groups[0]['lr']))
        if val_loss < best_loss - MIN_GAIN:
            best_loss = val_loss
            epochs_without_gain = 0
            best_state = {k: t.detach().clone() for k, t in net.state_dict().items()}
        else:
            epochs_without_gain += 1
            if epochs_without_gain >= SETTINGS['patience']:
                break
    net.load_state_dict(best_state)

BatchNorm needs two rows in training mode, hence the guard on a final one-row batch. Validation runs in 4,096-row chunks in evaluation mode. The best state is kept separately, so the overfitting after the best epoch (Figure 5.5) does not reach the saved network.

The original recipe stops after 5 epochs without gain and drops the learning rate after 3. On this problem that stopped training too early: with linear inputs, Merlin's test log p improved from −6.77 (patience 5, results_merlin_unbinned) to −5.01 (patience 30, results_merlin_unbinned_p30), and MOPED from −6.38 to −5.14. All later runs use MERLIN_PATIENCE=30 MERLIN_LR_PATIENCE=10, set in the PBS scripts (idark_jobs/train61k.pbs, idark_fisher/merlin_train.pbs). With these settings the Merlin networks at 61,440 rows train for 83 to 102 epochs (production, 20-draw whitening; 349 to 433 s per seed) and 73 to 104 epochs (420-draw whitening). The 40-band networks are trained differently, with sbi's own trainer (Adam, learning rate 7×10−4, batch 512, 10 % validation, gradient clipping), for about 340 to 500 epochs (explainer page, section 10), so the two families are not on a matched training recipe.

Three seeds (1, 2, 3) are trained for every configuration and combined as an equal-weight mixture, qens = (1/3) Σk qk, evaluated as a log-sum-exp of the seed log densities minus ln 3 (train_ensemble.py lines 359 to 376). The mixture is 0.7 to 1.1 nats better in test log p than a single Merlin seed (explainer page, section 10), because a truth in the tail of one seed still has density under the others. Errors quoted as "± " on test log p below are the standard deviation over the three seeds' own mean log p divided by √3.

5.8 Bounded (logit) and unbounded (linear) parameter spaces

The flow does not model θ directly. By default each parameter is mapped to its position t in the prior range and then through the logit, u = ln[t/(1 − t)]. The flat prior becomes a logistic distribution on the real line and every sample maps back inside the box: these are the bounded networks. Densities in θ need the Jacobian of the map:

t = (θ − θmin) / w,   u = ln[t / (1 − t)],   ln p(θ | x) = ln q(u | x) − Σi ln |dθi/dui|,   dθ/du = w σ(u) σ(−u)

(train_ensemble.py lines 60 to 83, σ the logistic function). Setting MERLIN_PARAM_SPACE=linear uses u = t instead, with a constant Jacobian, so the flow is free to put mass outside the box: the unbounded networks. They were first trained on 2026-10-01 (results_merlin_unbinned_asinh_p30_lin) because the logit space confines a posterior to the box by construction; in linear space, mass beyond a bound is the flow's continuation of the posterior past the edge, where there are no training simulations (merlin_compression.py docstring, lines 53 to 55). They show where an observation would pull if the box were not there.

5.10 Why 50-multipole bands: the fixed-sky fingerprint

Every one of the 64,440 training spectra was painted on the same HalfDome lightcone (realisation 100) and seen through the same mask (seed 12345). Training varies θ and the SO noise, never the halos or the window, so the network learns p(θ | D, H100, W12345), the posterior conditional on that one sky. Section 4 describes the tests in which B12 maps were painted on nine other lightcones (seeds 102 to 118) and seen through nine other masks, all with the same noise pair. The original unbounded Merlin, which places the seed-100 B12 sky at P0 = 12.23 and β = 9.66, moves the B12 maps of the nine other lightcones (seen through the training mask) to P0 = 49.89 ± 2.32 and β = 13.01 ± 0.21 (scatter of the posterior medians across the nine skies, not a posterior uncertainty; truth 18.1 and 4.35), and in the later 10 × 10 robustness grid excludes the truth from its 95 % joint region on every held-out sky (tsz_seed_offset_diagnosis_20261005/REPORT.md; tsz_robustness_methods_20261004/final_results/INTERPRETATION.md).

The spectra of the other skies are not very different. Relative to seed 100 they differ by 10.7 % (simulations) and 15.0 % (masks) below ℓ ≈ 580 and by less than 0.7 % above ℓ ≈ 2980 (INTERPRETATION.md). A pure amplitude change of +0.742 % in clean power barely moves P0 (12.43). The controlled perturbations of the seed-offset diagnosis locate the cause: smoothing the fractional clean-signal change of the other skies with a Gaussian of σℓ = 5 before adding it to the seed-100 spectrum brings all nine back to P0 between 12.13 and 15.46, while the removed fine residual alone keeps the shift (P0 = 40.09 for σℓ = 5 and 42.43 for σℓ = 50). The network reads structure that varies over a few multipoles.

The Fisher forecast shows the same thing from the information side (Figure 5.11). Its derivatives contain fine structure in ℓ that is identical whether computed with a step of 0.5 % or 1 % of the prior width: the correlation of the high-passed derivatives at the two step sizes is 1.000 for all eight non-amplitude parameters (results_fisher_b12_beam/fisher_forecast_b12.json, "derivative_check"). It is deterministic, not numerical noise: when a pressure parameter changes, the contribution of each individual halo of the fixed catalogue to each multipole changes in a fixed way. And it carries most of the local information. Low-passing the noise-scaled derivatives so that only periods above 50 multipoles survive raises the geometric-mean marginal Fisher width from 0.15 to 0.56 prior widths; averaging the data into bands of 50 raises it to 0.81 (bands of 2, 5, 10, 20 and 100 give 0.20, 0.29, 0.37, 0.52 and 1.18). Most of what a per-multipole method can learn at B12 is in structure finer than 50 multipoles, and that structure is, by the argument above, a fingerprint of the halos and mask of the training sky. It is real information about θ on that sky, but it does not carry over to another sky, where a network that relies on it is pulled off the truth (the fine-residual controls above). The derivatives were computed on the training sky only, so the statement that their fine structure differs from sky to sky is inferred from the data-side controls, not measured on the derivatives themselves.

Merlin is the compression most exposed to this, because its input is per-multipole and its PCA basis is fitted on the same sky. The 40 bands of width 200 average the fingerprint away. The question behind band50 was whether a compression that keeps the whole multipole range but discards structure finer than a chosen scale can keep most of Merlin's precision and lose the sky dependence.

5.11 band50: definition and construction

band50 replaces the 7,900 multipoles by 158 contiguous bands of 50 multipoles each, 80 to 129, 130 to 179, …, 7930 to 7979, every multipole entering exactly one band. Within a band the spectrum is averaged with weights 2ℓ + 1, the number of harmonic modes of each multipole on a full sky:

Db = Σℓ∈b (2ℓ+1) Dℓ / Σℓ∈b (2ℓ+1) = (A D)b,   A ∈ ℝ158×7900,   Cband = A C AT

The 2ℓ + 1 weight is a prescription, not inverse-covariance weighting under the actual mask, and it is not Gaussian smoothing with σℓ = 50, nor an average over 50 simulations (tsz_truth_mean_validation_20261006/README.md, "What band50 means"). Widths 25, 50 and 100 would give 316, 158 and 79 band powers; only 50 was trained. No band-width sweep was run, so 50 is a practical choice, not an optimised scale.

def band_operator():
    a = np.zeros((158, 7900))
    for i, (lo, hi) in enumerate(zip(EDGES[:-1], EDGES[1:])):
        m = (ELL >= lo) & (ELL < hi)
        a[i, m] = (2 * ELL[m] + 1) / np.sum(2 * ELL[m] + 1)
    assert np.allclose(a.sum(1), 1)
    return a


def noise_covariance(sd, rho, operator):
    """Band the saved beam-consistent SO covariance, preserving all fitted lag correlations."""
    diagonals, offsets = [sd**2], [0]
    for lag in range(1, len(rho)):
        values = rho[lag] * sd[lag:] * sd[:-lag]
        diagonals.extend([values, values]); offsets.extend([lag, -lag])
    cov = diags(diagonals, offsets, shape=(7900, 7900), format='csr')
    a = csr_matrix(operator)
    result = (a @ cov @ a.T).toarray()
    return (result + result.T) / 2

The operator rows sum to one (asserted). noise_covariance rebuilds the saved 420-draw lag model (standard deviations sd and lag correlations rho from the Fisher run) as a sparse banded matrix and forms A C AT, so the mask correlation between neighbouring bands is kept. This covariance serves the MOPED candidates; the Merlin candidate uses the noise-only one (next excerpt).

5.12 band50 results: the sky test

The robustness study crossed ten lightcones (100, 102, …, 118) with ten masks (12345 to 12354), all painted at the B12 parameters with the same noise pair, and compared 23 alternative compressions with the original unbounded Merlin (24 tested methods in all). Six simulations × five masks were used for selection ("discovery"); four held-out simulations at the training mask, five held-out masks on simulation 100 and their 4 × 5 intersection were used for validation. Two discrepancy measures were defined in units of hi, half the original model's 68 % width on the baseline sky, which is held fixed so that broadening a posterior cannot make it look better:

T = √⟨[(mci − θi*)/hi]2⟩c,i (truth displacement),   A = √⟨[(mci − m̄i)/hi]2⟩c,i (sky scatter),   W = exp⟨ln(wci/wciorig)⟩c,i (width ratio)

with mci the posterior median for sky c and parameter i and w the 68 % width. The selection rule, under the 25 % average-width budget confirmed by the user, was: among candidates that lower both T and A on the discovery skies and pass marginal and joint calibration screens, take the lowest √(T2 + A2) with W ≤ 1.25 (INTERPRETATION.md, "Metric definitions"). band50 Merlin won in all three arms. On the discovery skies (combined arm) it moved T from 1.560 to 1.134 and A from 0.498 to 0.077 at W = 1.199; the ℓ ≥ 580 control reached 1.175 and 0.147 at W = 1.250, just over the cap, and failed the joint calibration screen in the mask and combined arms (final_results/report/discovery_ranking.csv).

A note on a similarly named control. The joint report of 2026-10-05 (tsz_joint_report_20261005) used a "smooth_clean_50" input: the seed-100 observation plus the fractional mean clean difference of simulations 102 to 118 smoothed with a Gaussian of σℓ = 50 (control_definition.json), fed to the original frozen Merlin. band50 makes the posterior insensitive to the halo realisation and mask (held-out truth displacement −30 %, sky scatter −72 %, truth inside the 68 % joint region on all 100 skies) at a cost of about 46 % in posterior width across the prior and a drop in mean truth-mean Pearson r from 0.730 to 0.584, the level of 40 band powers. The evidence (this drop, the Fisher information by scale and the smooth/fine controls) indicates that most of Merlin's lead over 40 bands is information tied to the training sky.

5.14 Conclusions and open issues

The Merlin chain as implemented is sound and fully checked: every input can be rebuilt from a spectrum (the B12 observation through obs_beam_posteriors.py reproduces its stored network input exactly, maximum difference 0.0 in obs_posteriors_beam/summary_bounded.json, "checks"; the band models reconstruct with zero error), the whitening and PCA follow the original code's rules, and the bounded ensembles are calibrated to within a few per cent in marginal and TARP coverage. Of the steps, the asinh input scale and the training patience mattered most for the metrics; the whitening mattered at the 0.2 to 0.5 nat level and through which components survive PCA; the beam fix did not matter. The head adds about 0.05 in r and 1.6 nats in log p over plain whitened PCA.

The deeper result is about what Merlin's advantage consists of. Per-multipole inputs let it use the fine structure that the fixed HalfDome catalogue and mask imprint on the spectrum, which is real information about θ conditional on that sky but does not transfer to another HalfDome sky (measured, sections 5.10 and 5.12) and is not expected to transfer to FLAMINGO or to the real sky (not tested). band50 shows the trade-off directly: it removes most of the sky sensitivity and the accuracy drops to the 40-band level. For inference on any sky other than the training one, band50 (or 40 bands) is the defensible choice; the original Merlin numbers hold conditional on the training sky and are optimistic for any other.

Open issues. (1) The principled fix is to vary halo catalogues and masks across the training rows and test on whole held-out skies; band50 only avoids the fingerprint. (2) No band-width sweep: 25 and 100 were only illustrated, so the best scale between robustness and precision is unknown. (3) A zero-centred asinh for PCA and band inputs (the faint-sky compression) and a training recipe matched to the 40-band networks are untested. (4) The ℓ ≥ 580 control has no bounded companion. (5) band50 changes three things at once relative to the original (no PCA, banded whitening, retraining), so the measured cost is not the cost of averaging alone. (6) The B12 truth log p is one observation with seed errors of 0.05 to 0.98 nats and must not be used to rank methods. (7) Any transfer test on FLAMINGO maps needs the pixel-window treatment: the FLAMINGO map is a pixel average while the training signal is point-sampled, which obs_beam_posteriors.py handles only approximately with its L1_m9_pixwin variant (explainer page, section 13).
