3. 64k SBI: final results

This section establishes what the 64k flat-prior tSZ dataset supports when it is fed to neural posterior estimation (NPE): which version of the analysis is final and why, how each test metric is computed, how well the nine Battaglia et al. (2012, B12) pressure-profile parameters are recovered (correlations, widths, corner plots), whether the posteriors are calibrated (coverage, TARP, SBC), and how the results grow with the number of simulations. The short answer: with beam-consistent noise and 61,440 training simulations, Merlin gives the best marginal constraints on the HalfDome sky it was trained on (mean Pearson r 0.729) and calibrated joint posteriors, the 40-band network gives the highest joint log density of the truth (−2.35 nats against −2.96), and every network is still limited by the number of simulations, not by the SO noise. How much of Merlin's lead is specific to that one sky is the subject of sections 4 and 5.

3.1 What is inferred, from what

The simulator is the frozen dataset generator tSZ_64k_flat_prior_SO_baseline_deproj0/ (GitHub tag tsz64k-flat-prior-v1, commit b46ab65; not edited for this report). For each parameter vector θ it paints a Compton-y map of all 85,224,251 HalfDome lightcone halos with M200c ≥ 1012 M☉ (the catalogue masses, in M☉/h, are divided by h = 0.68 before the cut; engine/benchmark.jl, catalogue_pass!), using a generalised NFW (gNFW) electron-pressure profile. A gNFW profile is a broken power law in the scaled radius x = r/R200c: an inner slope γ inside the core radius xc and an outer slope set by β beyond it, multiplied by the self-similar pressure scale P200c of a halo of mass M200c (the mass inside the radius where the mean density is 200 times the critical density).

Pth(x) / P200c = P0 (x/xc)γ [1 + (x/xc)α]−β,   A = A0 (M200c / 1014 M☉)αmA (1+z)αzA for A ∈ {P0, xc, β}
P200c = G M200c 200 ρcrit(z) fb / (2 R200c),   Pe = 0.5176 Pth,   y = (σT / mec2) ∫ Pe dl

The nine inferred parameters are the three amplitudes P0, xc, β and their mass and redshift slopes αm and αz; α = 1 and γ = −0.3 are fixed. The factor 0.5176 converts thermal to electron pressure for a fully ionised hydrogen-helium gas with hydrogen mass fraction XH = 0.76: Pe/Pth = 2(XH + 1)/(5XH + 3). All nine have independent flat priors (P0 1 to 60, xc 0.025 to 4, β 2.8 to 16, αmP0 −0.6 to 1.5, αmxc −1 to 0.4, αmβ −0.2 to 0.4, αzP0 −6 to 0.5, αzxc −1.5 to 3, αzβ −0.5 to 2; bundle README). The B12 point used as the reference mock is (18.1, 0.497, 4.35, 0.154, −0.00865, 0.0393, −0.758, 0.731, 0.415). Gas is counted only inside a sphere of 4 R200c, so the line-of-sight integral is a chord of that sphere and y falls to zero at the projected radius 4 R200c.

The 65,536 parameter points are a scrambled Sobol sequence (a low-discrepancy design: every power-of-two prefix is itself balanced), and every row gets its own two noise seeds from a hash of its index:

LOW and HIGH are the prior box. design maps Sobol points linearly into the box (flat prior, no rejection). noise_seed gives the 131,072 split seeds, all distinct; they seed Julia 1.12.2's MersenneTwister, so the noise maps are reproducible from the row index alone (section 4 discusses the seeds and the mask).

Each painted map (raw HEALPix NSIDE 8192, harmonic transform to ℓmax 12287, 2 arcmin Gaussian beam, NSIDE 4096 output; NSIDE is the HEALPix resolution parameter, and NSIDE 4096 pixels are about 0.86 arcmin across) becomes an observation in save_observation. The noise is the Simons Observatory (SO) Large Aperture Telescope baseline noise curve of the tSZ internal-linear-combination (ILC) map with no extra foreground component deprojected ("Deproj-0"), file SO_LAT_Nell_T_atmv1_baseline_fsky0p4_ILC_tSZ.txt:

Line 23: one fixed apodised cap mask covering a sky fraction fsky = 0.4, 60 arcmin apodisation, seed 12345. Lines 34-37: two Gaussian SO baseline Deproj-0 noise maps from the row's two seeds. Lines 40-43: each split is mask × noise + masked signal, and the stored spectrum is the cross spectrum of the two splits. Because the two noise maps are independent, the cross removes on average the noise power Nℓ that an auto spectrum would carry, but keeps its scatter, so the stored spectrum can be negative where noise dominates. Each split carries the full SO Nℓ (split_Nell_multiplier = 1). The noise is added without the beam (the engine records noise_beam_applied = false), which is the production-noise issue that the beam-consistent correction of section 3.3 fixes at the spectrum level.

The collector stores Dℓ = ℓ(ℓ+1)Cℓ/2π for ℓ = 80 to 7979, 7,900 numbers per row. The collaborator's production run (Flatiron) finished 64,440 of the 65,536 rows; 274 batches of four rows (1,096 rows) failed and were never rerun there (SBI_analysis/tsz64k_sbi_local/final_dataset.py docstring and data/final_status/missing_batches.json; a disk-quota error according to the project notes). Those 1,096 rows were regenerated on idark with the same bundle and serve as an independent validation set (section 3.11). No mode-coupling correction is applied anywhere: training spectra and any observation pass through the same mask and estimator, so the inference never needs one.

function get_params(model::Battaglia16ThermalSZProfile{T}, M_200, z) where T
	z₁ = z + 1
	m = M_200 / (1e14M_sun)
	P₀ = powerlaw_value(model.P0, m, z₁)
	xc = powerlaw_value(model.x_c, m, z₁)
	α = powerlaw_value(model.alpha, m, z₁)
	β_raw = powerlaw_value(model.beta, m, z₁)
    γ = powerlaw_value(model.gamma, m, z₁)
    # Battaglia parameterizes the outer factor as (1 + x^α)^(-β_raw), while
    # generalized_nfw expects the asymptotic outer slope directly. Matching the
    # large-radius behavior x^(γ - αβ_raw) = x^(-β) gives β = αβ_raw - γ.
    β = α * β_raw - γ
    return (xc=T(xc), α=T(α), β=T(β), γ=T(γ), P₀=T(P₀))
end

Line 84 is the corrected conversion β = αβraw − γ (the asymptotic outer slope that generalized_nfw expects).

The cached quantity is the chord mean (chord integral divided by the chord length 2L); the painter multiplies it by the exact chord factor 2X√(1 − (θ/θmax)2). The wrapper still depends on the two XGPaint edits above: it reads the gNFW parameters through XGPaint.get_params (line 125, so the e67a8a1 β conversion applies) and the amplitude through the fork's tSZ prepare_profile_slice (projection_amplitude, line 104 of the same file). The shared-geometry painter (engine/benchmark.jl), the mask, the noise, the beam and the beam-consistent noise correction (SBI_analysis/tsz64k_sbi_local/beamfix/) are also project code, as is everything in the SBI pipeline below.

All 65,536 rows share one HalfDome sky: the halo positions, masses and redshifts never change, only the pressure profile and the noise. The test metrics below therefore measure how well the profile parameters are recovered on this sky. They contain no cosmic (sample) variance, and information that comes from this sky's specific halo pattern (section 3.9) would not transfer to FLAMINGO or to the real sky.

3.2 From a spectrum to a posterior

Neural posterior estimation. NPE trains a conditional density estimator qφ(θ | x) on pairs (θ, x) drawn from the prior and the simulator by minimising the mean of −log qφ(θ | x). That average equals the expected Kullback-Leibler divergence between the true posterior and qφ plus a constant, so a flexible enough qφ approaches p(θ | x) for every x at once ("amortised": a new posterior costs one network evaluation). The density estimator here is a masked autoregressive flow (MAF) from the sbi package: a chain of invertible maps from a standard Gaussian in which each parameter is shifted and scaled with coefficients predicted from the previous parameters and the conditioning input. 40 bins use MAF 128×5 (128 hidden units, 5 transforms), MOPED MAF 64×5, Merlin and PCA MAF 128×5 behind their own input layers. A neural spline flow (NSF), whose transforms are monotonic rational-quadratic splines rather than shifts and scalings, was tested as a capacity control.

Where the flow lives: logit space. The flow does not model θ but u = logit((θ − θmin)/w), w = θmax − θmin. A flat prior on θ becomes an independent standard logistic distribution on u, every sample maps back inside the prior box, and densities convert with the Jacobian of the map:

def to_u(theta, low, high):
    """Map parameters from the prior box to logit space.

    theta: (..., dim) parameters; low, high: (dim,) prior bounds. Returns u = logit((theta - low) / (high - low)),
    float64 of the same shape. A flat prior on theta becomes an independent standard logistic prior on u.
    """
    unit = np.clip((np.asarray(theta, dtype=np.float64) - low) / (high - low), UNIT_CLIP, 1 - UNIT_CLIP)
    # logit(s) = log s - log(1 - s); the clip keeps points on the box edges finite
    return np.log(unit) - np.log1p(-unit)


def from_u(u, low, high):
    """Inverse of to_u: theta = low + (high - low) * sigmoid(u), float64 of the shape of u (..., dim)."""
    return low + (high - low) / (1 + np.exp(-np.asarray(u, dtype=np.float64)))


def log_jacobian_theta_per_u(u, low, high):
    """log |d theta / d u| summed over parameters (theta-space density = u-space density / this).

    u: (..., dim) logit-space points; low, high: (dim,) prior bounds. Returns (...,) float64.
    """
    u = np.asarray(u, dtype=np.float64)
    # d theta / du = width * sigmoid(u) * sigmoid(-u), and -log sigmoid(u) = log(1 + e^-u) = logaddexp(0, -u)
    return np.sum(np.log(high - low) - np.logaddexp(0, u) - np.logaddexp(0, -u), axis=-1)

log_jacobian_theta_per_u is Σi log |dθi/dui| with dθ/du = w σ(u) σ(−u). Every log p of the truth below is the network's u-space log density minus this term, so all log densities are in θ units and directly comparable with the flat-prior value. The clip (UNIT_CLIP = 10−9) keeps truths on a box edge finite.

u = log[t/(1 − t)],  t = (θ − θmin)/w;    log pθ(θ | x) = log qu(u | x) − Σi log |dθi/dui|;    log pprior(θ) = −Σi log wi = −12.90 inside the box

A "bounded" network is one trained in logit space. An "unbounded" network (environment switch MERLIN_PARAM_SPACE=linear in merlin_compression.py) uses u = t, so posterior mass can leak past the prior edges. Section 3.7 shows why the bounded networks are the ones to use.

Ensembles. Each configuration is trained with three seeds, and the posterior is their equal-weight mixture. A truth in the tail of one seed's posterior still has density under the mixture, which raises the test log p over single seeds by 0.7 to 1.1 nats for Merlin and 1.1 to 1.7 for PCA (results_doc, section 10), and by 0.3 to 0.8 nats for the final 40-band ensemble (single seeds −3.14, −2.85, −2.66 against −2.35; lp_seeds in doc_metric_data/beam_bins40.npz).

        terms = []
        with torch.no_grad():
            for net in self.nets:
                chunks = [net.log_prob(torch.from_numpy(u[i:i + samples_per_call]), condition=x).numpy()
                          for i in range(0, u.shape[0], samples_per_call)]
                terms.append(np.concatenate(chunks, axis=0))
        # equal-weight mixture: log of the mean of the seed densities
        return np.logaddexp.reduce(np.stack(terms), axis=0) - np.log(len(self.nets))

log qens = logsumexpk log qk − log 3. Sampling draws n/3 points from each seed and shuffles them per row (sample_u, L334-L357), so 1,000 requested draws give 999.

Compressions. The four ways of turning 7,900 multipoles into the flow's conditioning input differ only in that input. 40 bins: averages of Dℓ over 200-multipole bands weighted by the number of modes 2ℓ + 1 (the last band is 100 wide), each passed through a signed asinh whose scale is the band's noise level and then z-scored. The noise level is the standard deviation of the band's split cross power at zero signal, Nb/√νb, where Nb is the per-split band noise power and νb the effective number of modes, both fitted from the training rows (sbi_final.py, fit_noise_model and bins40_scale). MOPED (Massively Optimised Parameter Estimation and Data compression): the fixed linear map at B12 from the 256-row diagnostic, one number per parameter, built from the derivatives of the mean spectrum and the noise covariance at the B12 point. PCA (principal component analysis): the multipoles are whitened with a noise covariance (L−1x with C = LLT, estimated with Oracle Approximating Shrinkage, OAS), projected on the 75 to 86 leading principal components of the whitened noise-free spectra, then put through a centred asinh and z-score. Merlin (Wernersson, Franco-Abellan & Cañas-Herrera 2026, arXiv:2609.24946, as cited in merlin_compression.py): the same PCA inputs passed through a learned head (2048 → 512 → 18 features) trained jointly with the flow. The asinh is linear within one noise unit and logarithmic beyond, which keeps rows that differ by many decades of tSZ power on an O(1) input scale. Section 5 describes Merlin, the whitening and the smoothing tests in full; here only their results are compared.

3.3 Which version is final, and why

The analysis went through several corrections. The table lists the steps that changed the numbers. From the third row on, every step uses the same nested-training-set protocol: a fixed random permutation (seed 0) sets aside 3,000 test rows, a second permutation (seed 1) orders the remaining 61,440, and the training set of size N is the first N rows of that order.

The first five rows are 40-band networks. The first two rows use a different, smaller held-out set, so their log p is not comparable with the others; their TARP values are. The box-space networks (2-seed ensembles, flows trained directly on θ and rejection-sampled into the box) covered only 51 % of truths with their 68 % regions and 84 to 86 % with their 95 % regions; the logit-space ensembles on the same rows were calibrated, which is why everything after that step is bounded. The median-scale asinh put the lower half of the prior, B12 included, into the linear part of the asinh where differences are tiny compared with the spread that sets the z-score; the noise-level scale moved the 40-band log p from −4.61 to −2.39 nats, more than a doubling of the training set is worth.

Beam-consistent noise. The SO noise curves are beam-deconvolved, so on a map they belong under the same beam as the signal. The engine beamed the signal and added the noise unbeamed, which overstates the noise × noise term by 1/Bℓ2 and the signal × noise term by 1/Bℓ, where Bℓ is the transfer function of the 2 arcmin Gaussian beam (Bℓ < 1, falling with ℓ). Write Css for the masked clean signal spectrum, Cnn for the cross spectrum of the two masked noise maps, Csn for the signal × noise cross terms and Cnoisy for the stored split cross spectrum, so that Cnoisy = Css + Csn + Cnn. Rather than repaint, the noise maps were regenerated on idark from their seeds, giving Cnn per row, and the spectrum was corrected as Cbeam = Css + Bℓ(Cnoisy − Css − Cnn) + Bℓ2Cnn (beamfix/beamfix.py, function correct). The band noise rms after the correction is unchanged at low ℓ and 13 % of the production value in the last band (results_doc section 3). This is a project-side correction, not an XGPaint change. Its effect on the SBI is small: in the staged series the paired log p gain over production noise at N = 61,440 is +0.35 nats with a row error of 0.02 (series.json, paired_log_p_noise), but the training scatter of a 40-band ensemble is larger (its three single seeds give −3.14, −2.85 and −2.66), and the Merlin size series with and without the correction (B12 whitening) agree within 0.04 nats from 2,000 to 32,000 simulations and differ by +0.11 at 61,440 (metrics_merlin.json of results_merlin_unbinned_asinh_p30[_N*] and results_merlin_unbinned_beam_asinh_p30[_N*]). It is kept because it is the physically correct noise, not because it improves the numbers.

Noise-only whitening. Merlin and PCA need a whitening covariance. The first choice was 20 SO noise draws of the B12 mock (OAS shrinkage 0.95, so the correlation between neighbouring multipoles was shrunk away), which also carries the B12 signal × noise term and so depends on a fiducial model. The final choice uses the regenerated noise × noise spectra of the 61,440 training rows (shrinkage 0.17, lag-1 correlation 0.455), which needs no fiducial (results_doc section 5). For Merlin it is at least as good: its log p goes from −3.28 to −2.96 and its mean r rises by 0.0080 ± 0.0018 with all nine parameters positive. For PCA the change is within the errors (mean r −0.0020 ± 0.0038; log p −4.33 ± 0.13 to −4.56 ± 0.20; results_doc section 11.8 and the metrics files below).

The final set, used for every figure from 3.1 to 3.10, is therefore: beam-consistent noise, the full 61,440 training rows, bounded three-seed ensembles, Merlin and PCA with noise-only whitening, and the 40-band network of the last stage of the staged beam series, all evaluated on the same 3,000 test rows. Three things decide "final": it is the physically correct observation model (beam-consistent noise), it uses every simulation produced, and it is fiducial-free and calibrated. A Merlin variant whitened with a 420-draw B12 covariance model reaches −2.82 ± 0.08 nats, but it depends on the B12 fiducial and is used here only for the Fisher comparison in Figure 3.11.

3.4 How each metric is computed

Every stored metric comes from one function, run on the 3,000 test rows with 999 posterior draws per row (fixed torch and numpy seeds, so the draws are reproducible and the per-row files written later reproduce the stored r exactly):

    torch.manual_seed(1)
    s, _, _ = ens.posterior(x, samples, rng=np.random.default_rng(1))          # (samples, rows, 9)
    mean = s.mean(0)
    q16, q84 = np.quantile(s, [.16, .84], axis=0)
    # density of the truth: the network's u-space density divided by |d theta / d u|
    u_t = to_u(truth, low, high)
    lp = ens.log_prob_u(u_t[None], x)[0] - log_jacobian_theta_per_u(u_t, low, high)
    torch.manual_seed(1)
    truth_t = torch.tensor(truth, dtype=torch.float32)
    references = get_tarp_references(truth_t)                  # random reference points (torch seed 1)
    ecp, alpha = _run_tarp(torch.tensor(s, dtype=torch.float32), truth_t, references, l2, None, True)
    c68, c95 = np.interp([.68, .95], np.ravel(alpha.numpy()), np.ravel(ecp.numpy()))
    # the prior's RMSE: the posterior mean replaced by the centre of the box
    prior_rmse = np.sqrt(np.mean(((truth - (low + high) / 2) / width) ** 2, axis=0))
    flat_edge = 2 * EDGE
    metrics = dict(mean_log_prob_theta=float(lp.mean()),
                   mean_log_prob_theta_sem=float(lp.std() / np.sqrt(len(lp))),
                   tarp68=float(c68), tarp95=float(c95),
                   marginal_coverage68=np.mean((truth >= q16) & (truth <= q84), axis=0).tolist(),
                   median_width68_over_prior=np.median((q84 - q16) / width, axis=0).tolist(),
                   pearson_r=[float(np.corrcoef(truth[:, i], mean[:, i])[0, 1]) for i in range(9)],
                   rmse_over_prior_rmse=(np.sqrt(np.mean(((mean - truth) / width) ** 2, axis=0))
                                         / prior_rmse).tolist(),
                   median_width68_over_flat=np.median((q84 - q16) / (FLAT_WIDTH68 * width), axis=0).tolist(),
                   edge_mass_ratio=(edge_fraction(s, low, high) / flat_edge).tolist(),
                   edge_mass_ratio_truth=(edge_fraction(truth, low, high) / flat_edge).tolist())
    return metrics, lp

L328: posterior draws s (draws × rows × 9). L333: log p of the truth from the u-space density and the Jacobian. L336-L338: TARP with sbi's implementation. L345: marginal 68 % coverage; L346 and L350: widths; L347: Pearson r; L348-L349: RMSE relative to the prior centre; L351: the edge-mass ratio (share of draws in the outer 1 % of each prior range over its flat value of 2 %, a check for pile-ups at the box edges).

Log p of the truth

⟨log p⟩ = (1/N) Σj [ log qens(uj | xj) − log |dθ/du|uj ],    SEMseeds = sdk(⟨log p⟩k) / √3

The mean log posterior density of the true parameters is a proper scoring rule for the full nine-dimensional posterior: it is maximised in expectation only by the true posterior, and it rewards putting density where the truth is jointly in all nine parameters, including along narrow degeneracies. A flat posterior scores −12.90 nats. One nat is a factor e in density, so "1.3 nats per doubling" means that each doubling of N multiplies the density at the truth by about e1.3 ≈ 3.7 on average (in the geometric-mean sense). It is the most sensitive metric and also the most fragile: an overconfident miss costs that row tens of nats (the worst Merlin row scores −34.7; results_doc section 12), so a minority of such rows can move the mean.

Pearson r of truth against posterior mean, and RMSE

ri = corrj(θji, μji),    RMSEi / RMSEprior = √⟨(μji − θji)2⟩j / √⟨(ci − θji)2⟩j,  ci = (θimin + θimax)/2

For each parameter, r is the correlation across the 3,000 test rows between the true value and the posterior mean μ. It measures how well the posterior mean tracks the truth, ignoring the width. r is unchanged by any linear rescaling or offset of the posterior means, so a uniform shrinkage towards the prior centre would not lower it. What lowers r is that rows with weak data all collapse onto the prior centre whatever their true value, which turns the truth-mean cloud horizontal (Figure 3.2, bottom). r is therefore a measure of information, not of bias; bias shows up in the RMSE and in the coverage. RMSE ratio 1 means nothing was learned (always guessing the prior centre). For a flat prior the prior-centre RMSE is w/√12.

68 % width

Wi = medianj (q84,ji − q16,ji) / (0.6827 wi)

The median over test rows of the central 68 % interval, in units of the 68 % interval of a flat posterior (0.6827 of the prior range). A flat posterior scores about 1.

Marginal coverage

The share of test rows whose truth lies inside its central 68 % (or 95 %) interval. A calibrated posterior gives 0.68 (0.95), within a 95 % binomial range of ±1.96√(0.68·0.32/3000) = ±0.017 for 3,000 rows. Coverage above 0.68 means intervals that are too wide (conservative); below means too narrow (overconfident). Width and coverage must be read together.

TARP: coverage of the joint posterior

fj = (1/n) Σs 1[ d(θ(s)j, θrefj) < d(θj, θrefj) ],    ECP(α) = (1/N) Σj 1[ fj < α ]

TARP (Tests of Accuracy with Random Points, Lemos et al. 2023) checks the nine-dimensional posterior at once. For each test row a reference point θref is drawn uniformly within the range of the test truths, all parameters are scaled to that range, and fj is the fraction of the row's posterior draws that lie closer (Euclidean distance) to the reference point than the truth does. Each reference point defines a ball around it; for a calibrated posterior the truth's position within these balls is uniform, f is uniform on [0, 1], and the expected coverage probability ECP(α) lies on the diagonal. The implementation is sbi 0.27's _run_tarp (histogram of f with 300 bins for 3,000 rows, cumulative sum); the stored tarp68 and tarp95 are the curve at α = 0.68 and 0.95. The grey band in the figures is the 95 % Kolmogorov-Smirnov band ±1.358/√3000 = ±0.025.

Simulation-based calibration (SBC)

    x, truth = ens.f['x_held'], ens.f['theta_held']
    torch.manual_seed(1)                                   # the draws of sbi_final.row_metrics
    s, _, _ = ens.posterior(x, samples, rng=np.random.default_rng(1))       # (samples, rows, 9)
    q = np.quantile(s, [.025, .16, .5, .84, .975], axis=0)
    out = dict(truth=truth, mean=s.mean(0), q025=q[0], q16=q[1], median=q[2], q84=q[3], q975=q[4])
    if ranks:
        out['rank'] = np.sum(s < truth[None], axis=0)          # (rows, 9): samples below the truth

The rank of the truth is the number of the row's 999 draws below it, per parameter. If the posterior is calibrated, the truth is just one more draw and the ranks are uniform on 0 to 999. The figures show the empirical CDF (ECDF) of rank/999 minus the uniform CDF: a hump below then above zero means ranks cluster in the middle (posterior too wide), the opposite S means too narrow, a one-sided bulge means a bias.

Interval score

ISα = (u − l) + (2/α)(l − θ) 1[θ < l] + (2/α)(θ − u) 1[θ > u],   α = 0.32,  in units of the prior width

Width and coverage pull in opposite directions, so neither ranks two methods alone. The interval score is a proper score for the central 68 % interval [l, u]: its width plus a penalty proportional to how far the truth falls outside (lower is better; doc_figures.py:L541-L545). A flat posterior scores about 0.84.

Comparing two networks on the same rows

def pairs_table(mset, boot=2000):
    """Paired differences between compressions on the same test rows: mean r, log p, interval score, with errors."""
    rows = per_row(mset)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
    out = []
    for a, b, name in (('bins40', 'merlin', 'Merlin − 40 bins'), ('pca', 'merlin', 'Merlin − PCA'),
                       ('bins40', 'pca', 'PCA − 40 bins')):
        if a not in rows or b not in rows:
            continue
        A, B = rows[a], rows[b]
        assert np.array_equal(A['truth'], B['truth'])
        t = A['truth']
        n = len(t)
        dr = (pearson(t, B['mean']) - pearson(t, A['mean'])).mean()
        bs = []
        for _ in range(boot):
            i = rng.integers(0, n, n)
            bs.append((pearson(t[i], B['mean'][i]) - pearson(t[i], A['mean'][i])).mean())
        sa, sb = pearson(t[None], A['seed_mean']).mean(1), pearson(t[None], B['seed_mean']).mean(1)
        e_r = np.hypot(np.std(bs, ddof=1), np.sqrt(sa.var(ddof=1) / 3 + sb.var(ddof=1) / 3))
        dl = B['lp'] - A['lp']
        la, lb = A['lp_seeds'].mean(1), B['lp_seeds'].mean(1)
        e_l = np.hypot(dl.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(n), np.sqrt(la.var(ddof=1) / 3 + lb.var(ddof=1) / 3))
        di = row_interval_score(B) - row_interval_score(A)
        out.append([name, f'{dr:+.3f} ± {e_r:.3f}', f'{dl.mean():+.2f} ± {e_l:.2f}',
                    f'{di.mean():+.3f} ± {di.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(n):.3f}'])

Differences between two networks are much smaller than the test-set scatter of either, but most of that scatter is shared because both see the same rows. The r difference is bootstrapped with the same resampled rows for both networks (seed 7, 2,000 resamples) and the seed-to-seed scatter of r (from each seed's own posterior means) is added in quadrature. The log p difference uses per-row differences plus the seed scatter. The interval-score error is the paired row error only (per-seed quantiles were not stored).

3.6 Widths and marginal coverage

Narrower is only better if coverage holds. Merlin's intervals are 0.55 of the flat width on the median against 0.78 for 40 bins, and its coverage is close to nominal, so most of the narrowing is real. Its undercoverage grows with sky brightness: the 68 % coverage over all nine parameters is 0.69 to 0.71 for skies below 1× B12 and 0.64 above 1000× B12 (95 %: 0.93), while for P0 and αmP0 it is low in most brightness bins (0.60 to 0.71; coverage_by_brightness in scripts/sbi64k/final_calibration_numbers.json). The interval score, which trades width against misses, ranks the three in the same order as r: Merlin 0.526, PCA 0.580, 40 bins 0.656, with a paired Merlin − 40 bins difference of −0.130 ± 0.003.

3.7 Joint calibration: TARP, SBC, bounded and unbounded

TARP and SBC agree: the final Merlin ensemble is calibrated jointly (TARP inside the band at every level) and close to calibrated per parameter, with P0 and αmP0 slightly overconfident (68 % coverage 0.643 and 0.649; three SBC curves just outside the band); PCA and 40 bins err on the safe side. Over the whole training-size range the 40-band TARP stayed at 71 to 74 % (68 % level) and 96 to 97 % (95 % level) in both the median-scale and noise-scale runs (results_final/convergence_table.md, results_final_noise/convergence_table.md), so the calibration does not depend on N; only the sharpness does.

Why the unbounded networks look sharper but are worse: a flow in linear space cannot produce a density that ends at a prior edge, so a posterior that is flat or piles up against a bound becomes a hump with tails past the bound. On the beam-consistent set the unbounded Merlin keeps r (0.729) but its coverage drops to 0.627 and its TARP to 0.635 / 0.953, and 33 to 34 % of its probability lies outside the prior box (results_doc section 9; results_merlin_unbinned_beam_nnwhite_asinh_p30_lin/metrics_merlin.json). In the SBC of the production-noise networks every unbounded network exceeds the 95 % band (largest |ECDF − uniform| 0.048 for Merlin against a band of 0.025), while the bounded Merlin stays within or near it (largest 0.032; results_publication/publication_numbers.json, sbc_unbounded and sbc_bounded). The bounded PCA and MOPED networks in the same file also exceed the band (largest 0.048 and 0.046), so SBC alone separates the two parametrisations clearly only for Merlin; the decisive evidence is the coverage, the TARP and the probability outside the box. The bounded networks are therefore the ones used for all constraints; the unbounded ones are kept only as a diagnostic of where the data push past the prior (section 3.12).

Three regimes. Faint skies (below 0.1× B12): Merlin and PCA stay near the prior while 40 bins still reaches −6.1 at 0.01 to 0.1×. The PCA and Merlin asinh is centred on the pool median, a sky about 14 times brighter than B12, so the projections of a faint sky on the first two principal components lie about 1,300 and 2,100 noise units from the centre (24 to 270 on components 3 to 6; results_doc section 7), in the logarithmic part of the asinh; a tenfold change in a faint sky then moves the input by only a few thousandths of its unit scale. The 40-band asinh is centred on zero and keeps such skies distinguishable. Skies of 0.1 to 1× B12 carry the largest share (0.39 nats): here Merlin's marginals are better (RMSE 0.214 against 0.234 of the prior width) but 40 bins gives the higher log p for 83 % of these rows, i.e. its joint posterior follows the narrow parameter degeneracies more closely, which log p rewards and one-parameter metrics do not see. The brightest skies (above 1000×): Merlin's intervals are narrow (width 0.43 against 0.69) but contain the truth only 64 % of the time at 68 %, and log p punishes the misses hard. Over all rows Merlin has the higher log p on 45 % of test rows but the lower mean.

These are observations, not established causes. The two network families were trained with different recipes (40 bins with sbi's trainer for 340 to 436 epochs; Merlin and PCA with AdamW for about 100), and log p has not levelled off with N (section 3.10), so log p reacts more to such differences than the marginal metrics do. A Merlin run with a zero-centred asinh and a matched trainer would test how much of the gap is the compression and how much the recipe; it has not been run.

The physics of the ridges: a larger core radius xc spreads the pressure outwards, and a steeper outer slope β cuts it back, so xc and β trade off at nearly fixed projected profile; the same holds for their mass and redshift slopes. The amplitude P0 trades off against its redshift slope because the power is dominated by halos over a limited redshift range. The posterior's position along the xc-β ridge is set by prior volume: the box extends to β = 16 and xc = 4, and many large-β, large-xc profiles fit the B12 spectrum once P0 is rescaled. In the earlier study on the 25,944 rows then available, each row's noise-free 40-band spectrum was rescaled by the exact factor that a change of P0 produces and compared with the noise-free B12 spectrum, with χ2 summed over the 40 bands in units of the scatter of 20 SO noise draws of the B12 sky (the noisy B12 mock itself has χ2 = 48 against the noise-free spectrum). The 25 rows with χ2 < 100 had a median β of 11.6 (range 4.3 to 15.8). The networks of that study were MOPED logit-space ensembles (results_logit/). Their β posterior conditional on the other eight parameters at their true values peaked at 4.38 (noise-trained network, noisy mock) and 4.48 (noise-free-trained network, noise-free spectrum), close to the input 4.35, so the network does respond to β when the degenerate directions are fixed. Feeding the noise-trained network the noise-free B12 spectrum gave the same marginal (β median 11.26 against 11.23 for the noisy mock), and re-beaming the noise-free spectrum from 2 arcmin to FWHM 1.8 to 2.2 arcmin moved the median only between 11.31 and 11.12 (input_scan; all numbers from results_logit/beta_bias.json). The high marginal β is therefore a degeneracy plus prior-volume effect, not a noise or beam artefact.

The boxed Fisher forecast. The Fisher matrix F = JTC−1J (J the derivatives of the spectrum with respect to θ at B12, C the noise covariance) gives the Gaussian posterior an ideal unbiased analysis would reach if the spectrum were linear in θ. Multiplying that Gaussian by the flat prior box gives the "boxed Fisher" posterior. The first version, inside sbi_final.py checks, used the 40 bands:

    for level, cov in (('baseline', C), ('goal', C * np.outer(sd, sd))):
        F = J.T @ np.linalg.solve(cov, J) * np.outer(width, width)                   # prior-width units
        lam, vec = np.linalg.eigh((F + F.T) / 2)
        order = np.argsort(lam)[::-1]
        lam, vec = lam[order], vec[:, order]                # best-measured direction first
        base = vec if base is None else base
        sigma = 1 / np.sqrt(lam)
        rng = np.random.default_rng(4)
        # the Gaussian posterior centred on the truth, truncated to the unit box
        inside, quad, proj = 0, 0.0, []
        for _ in range(draws // chunk):
            g = rng.standard_normal((chunk, 9))
            x = mu + (g * sigma) @ vec.T
            ok = np.all((x > 0) & (x < 1), axis=1)
            inside += int(ok.sum())
            quad += float(np.sum(g[ok] ** 2))
            proj.append((x[ok] - mu) @ base)
        # entropy of the truncated Gaussian: log Z + log det(2 pi Sigma)/2 + E[chi^2 | inside]/2
        log_norm = 0.5 * np.sum(np.log(2 * np.pi * sigma ** 2))
        H = np.log(inside / draws) + log_norm + 0.5 * quad / inside
        # log Z(m) for MLEs m scattered around the truth (floored at half a sample)
        log_z = []
        for _ in range(centres):
            m = mu + (rng.standard_normal(9) * sigma) @ vec.T
            y = m + (rng.standard_normal((per_centre, 9)) * sigma) @ vec.T
            log_z.append(np.log(max(np.mean(np.all((y > 0) & (y < 1), axis=1)), 0.5 / per_centre)))
        # unit-box densities to theta units: subtract sum log width
        out[level] = dict(sigma_eig=sigma.tolist(), inside_fraction=inside / draws,
                          std_along_baseline_eigenvectors=np.concatenate(proj).std(0).tolist(),
                          entropy_theta=float(H + np.sum(np.log(width))),
                          expected_log_prob_truth_theta=float(-log_norm - 4.5 - np.mean(log_z)
                                                              - np.sum(np.log(width))),
                          expected_log_prob_truth_sem=float(np.std(log_z) / np.sqrt(centres)))

With the 40 bands, the boxed Fisher expects log p(θB12) = +7.98 nats at SO baseline noise (+8.71 at SO goal noise), while the 40-band NPE at 61,440 gave −6.29 for the B12 truth averaged over 21 noise draws (median-scale run; results_final/checks.json). Measured from the prior (−12.90), the NPE has 6.6 of the roughly 20.9 nats the ideal would have. The gap sits in five tight Fisher directions: the Fisher eigen-widths are 5×10−5, 5×10−4, 0.008, 0.051 and 0.076 prior widths, while the NPE's posterior standard deviations along the same directions are 0.12 to 0.29; the four loose directions (eigen-widths 0.41 to 10.7) are prior-limited for both. The later, calibrated Fisher of all 7,900 multipoles (420 noise realisations of the B12 sky; Richardson derivatives, i.e. finite differences at steps h and 2h combined as (4Jh − J2h)/3; a covariance model calibrated on held-out realisations; fisher_forecast_b12.py) is the grey region of Figure 3.11. It is much tighter than anything a 40-band or PCA analysis can reach (marginal Fisher sd 1.1 to 2.7 prior widths for the 40 bands against 0.09 to 0.23 for all multipoles, calibrated; results_fisher_b12_beam/fisher_forecast_b12.json). That information lives in multipole-to-multipole fine structure of the derivatives: smoothing the derivatives over ±50 multipoles inflates the marginal sd to 0.73 to 1.78 prior widths. Its h and 2h derivatives agree to coherence 1.0000 in every period band (derivative_coherence_by_period), so the fine structure is a deterministic response of the simulator, not finite-difference noise; it is attributed to the one fixed HalfDome sky, whose halo pattern imprints structure in ℓ that does not average out. Merlin's PCA inputs keep part of it (Fisher marginal sd 0.39 to 1.01 prior widths for Merlin's PCA inputs, between the two cases above), which plausibly explains why Merlin and PCA beat 40 bins on the marginals. Sections 4 and 5 test this directly: when the same pressure law is painted on other HalfDome lightcones or seen through other masks, the Merlin posterior median of P0 moves from about 12 to 45-54 on the other lightcones and to 24-46 through the other masks against a truth of 18.1 (section 4 summary), and a 50-multipole-band variant (band50) removes most of the shift at the price of wider intervals. The metrics of this section are therefore metrics on the training sky.

3.10 Convergence with the number of simulations

The saturating fit is how "no flattening" is decided:

The model is L∞ − A (N/104)−α with the asymptote allowed up to 20 nats above the last point. In every variant of results_final_noise/convergence.json the fit ends at that upper bound (fit_at_bound true for Linf), and in results_final (median scale) L∞ = 15.39 = −4.61 + 20 is also the bound: the data show no curvature that would locate an asymptote. The log-linear slope against log2N (sbi_final.py:L868-L873) is therefore the honest summary.

On the 64k prior, every network gains 1.3 to 1.5 nats per doubling of the training set, and the last step, from 32,000 to 61,440 simulations, gave 0.95 to 2.1 nats per doubling depending on the network (log p difference divided by log2(61,440/32,000); scripts/sbi64k/logp_gain_per_doubling.json). Over the same range the 40-band TARP stays at 0.71 to 0.74 (68 %), the mean r of the first, median-scale 40-band run rose from 0.432 to 0.552, and Merlin's from 0.578 to 0.721 (B12 whitening). Only the narrower, older 524k set shows the expected bend, with local gains of 2.7 to 3.7 nats per doubling between 8,192 and 65,536 simulations falling to about 1.1 nats by 513,042 simulations; that set has a prior 1.8 to 7.6 times narrower per parameter (width_ratio_64k_over_524k in results_final/fig_correlation_prior_comparison.json) and the same noise maps in every row, so it suggests, but does not show, that the 64k curves would also bend somewhere above 105 simulations.

Noise is not the limit. In the median-scale run, training without noise gained +0.47 ± 0.27 nats at 61,440, Gaussian noise at the baseline level +0.30 ± 0.38 and at the SO goal level −0.12 ± 0.21, all within about one doubling of N (results_final/checks.json, differences_to_bins40_noisy). With the noise-level asinh the noise level matters a little more: no noise reaches −1.54 against −2.39 for map noise at 61,440 (Figure 3.13), and in the staged beam series the paired clean − noisy difference is +0.62 nats at 61,440 (row error 0.03, training scatter not included; series.json). Even that is less than the 1.5 nats one more doubling of simulations would give. The network is a secondary limit. MAF 256×8 adds +0.73 ± 0.23 nats at 32,000 and +0.86 ± 0.22 at 61,440 over MAF 128×5, and NSF 128×5 +0.65 ± 0.21 at 61,440 (checks.json). The larger MAF was trained at only those two sizes; its step from 32,000 to 61,440 (+1.02 nats) is similar to that of MAF 128×5 (+0.90), so the gain from capacity does not shrink as N grows. A bigger network is worth half to two thirds of a doubling of data (0.65 to 0.86 nats against 1.27), and both help. Neither comes close to the tight Fisher directions: the B12 posterior sd along the five tightest directions shrinks only from 0.19-0.32 to 0.12-0.29 prior widths between 2,000 and 61,440 simulations and stays at 0.12-0.29 with MAF 256×8 or NSF 128×5 (b12_std_along_fisher_eigenvectors in checks.json), against Fisher widths of 5×10−5 to 0.08. The simulations are the main limit, which is the working conclusion rather than a proof: log p keeps rising with N at a similar rate (1.3 to 1.5 nats per doubling) for every network, and the remaining information (section 3.9) sits in a few very narrow directions of parameter space that a density estimator can only resolve with many more training points.

3.11 Independent validation on the 1,096 idark rows

The idark rows were painted by a different machine and job system from the training rows. Their log p agrees with the test-row log p to −0.04 nats on average over the 33 ensembles (−0.10 to +0.03 for single ensembles, against row standard errors of 0.06 to 0.14 on the 1,096 rows), which shows that the metrics are not tuned to the 3,000 test rows and that the regenerated rows are consistent with the production rows. The validation rows are not a uniform draw in P0 (Kolmogorov-Smirnov p = 0.005 against the flat prior), while the other eight parameters are consistent with flat (p ≥ 0.99; prior_ks_pvalues in validation.json). The lost batches are not a random subset of the design: they come in 61 runs of consecutive design rows between rows 7,456 and 60,687 (data/rows_validation_1096/row_id.npy), so their parameters need not be uniform; why only P0 deviates was not investigated. The final beam-consistent networks have not been run on these rows.

3.12 Open issues

  • Simulation-limited. log p rises 1.3 to 1.5 nats per doubling with no measurable flattening at 61,440 simulations on the 64k prior; more simulations (or truncated sequential rounds around a given observation) are the main lever. Merlin passes the final 40-band mean r (0.590) with 4,000 simulations and PCA with 8,000, so they are the efficient choice for new runs.
  • One fixed sky. All metrics are conditional on the single HalfDome halo catalogue and mask. The all-multipole information is a fixed-sky fingerprint and does not transfer to other skies (sections 4 and 5: other lightcones and masks shift the Merlin posterior; band50 is the mitigation tested). A training set with varied lightcones and masks, or a compression restricted to smooth spectral structure, is needed before posteriors on FLAMINGO or real data can be trusted at the precision quoted here.
  • Merlin's two weak regimes. Near-prior posteriors for faint skies (the asinh is centred on the pool median) and undercoverage for skies above 1000× B12. An untested remedy: a zero-centred asinh for the PCA and Merlin inputs, with the 40-band trainer.
  • Different trainers for the 40-band and the Merlin/PCA networks; a like-for-like log p comparison needs a matched recipe.
  • Prior edges. The αzP0 prior (−6 to 0.5) is wide on the negative side and 48 % of the test truths lie below −3. For truths in [−1, 0) and [0, 0.5) the final Merlin posterior means are low by 0.46 and 0.68 and the 68 % coverage is 0.62 and 0.45 (40 bins: −0.91 and 0.51 in the top bin; scripts/sbi64k/final_calibration_numbers.json). Part of this is the expected pull of a weakly informed posterior towards the prior centre (Bayesian calibration holds on average over the prior, not for each truth value), and it is consistent with the B12 posterior of αzP0 sitting below its input value (Merlin median −1.23 against −0.758). The unbounded networks are the diagnostic of where the data push past the box.
  • Validation set evaluated only with the median-scale ensembles of results_final (the four 40-band variants and MOPED, production noise), not with the final beam-consistent networks.
