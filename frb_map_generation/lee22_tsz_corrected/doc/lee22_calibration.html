Lee22 calibration in HalfDome

How the Lee et al. (2022, MNRAS 517, 5303) IllustrisTNG electron density and pressure fits are corrected before painting HalfDome FRB dispersion measures, kSZ and tSZ maps. Status 2026-09-30.

In short

M1, the bookkeeping error. Lee22's extraction code divides particle sums by comoving shell volumes but normalises by the physical critical density. The printed fits are therefore low by a3 (density) and a2 (pressure), with a = 1/(1+z). Undoing it multiplies the density by (1+z)3 and the pressure by (1+z)2.

Fn and FP, what remains. After undoing M1 the printed density is still 5.1–5.9 times the TNG gas, the pressure is right (FP = 1.00 ± 0.05). Both follow from the code units: the density was counted per (ckpc/h)3 and normalised outside the code, leaving 1/h3 next to the a3, plus 1/XH2 from the printed n200; the pressure was normalised inside the code, where h cancels. Fn = 1/(h3XH2) = 5.57, no fitted constant.

What HalfDome uses. Pressure: Lee22 × (1+z)2. Density: Lee22 × [0.6774 (1+z)]3 XH2. The empirical corrections used on the way (C(z) = 0.284 (1+z)2.48 from a TNG DM catalogue whose "1013–1014" window likely contains clusters, and Fn = 5.4 from the CAMELS gas) are not final products.

Consequences. FRB halo DM: the previous ×0.6016 density was right only near z = 0.35; the derived density reproduces TNG's halo DM to 3 % in 1013–1014.95 M☉ (analytic halo model, once the TNG catalogue's mass window is read correctly). tSZ: Lee22 × (1+z)2 is 2.3–3× FLAMINGO's y power, mostly from its extrapolation above 1014.8 h-1M☉; with Battaglia12 there it is 1.15–1.5×. Against Takahashi+25 every corrected pair is now acceptable (χ² 20–24 / 10–23 vs 58.8 / 119.4 before).

1. The Lee22 fits as printed

Lee22 fit the mean electron density and electron pressure of TNG300 halos (1013–1014.8 h-1M☉, 0.04–1.34 R200c, 20 snapshots to z = 2) with one generalised NFW shape. We use the fits without concentration (Tables 7 and 8):

ne(x) / n200 = n0 (x/xc)γ [1 + (x/xc)α]−β′,   Pe(x) / P200 = P0 (x/xc)γ [1 + (x/xc)α]−β x = r/R200c, α = 1, γ = −0.3; each amplitude A = A0 (M/Mcut)αm (1+z)αz, broken at Mcut = 1013.61 h-1M☉; e.g. n0 = 6.8 (M/Mcut)0.68(1+z)−2.11, P0 = 2.8 (M/Mcut)0.80…1.08(1+z)−1.89
n200 = 200 ρcr(z) fb / (XH mp),   P200 = 200 G M200 ρcr(z) fb / (2 R200) physical ρcr(z) and physical R200c, as stated in the paper (eq. 9–10)

Our transcription is exact: it reproduces the paper's own fit curves (vector paths extracted from the arXiv source figures) to 2–3 %. Fig. 1 replicates the paper's Fig. 2 and Fig. 5 with the paper's own TNG profile, digitized from the vector figures together with its error bars. The residual rows use the paper's definitions, which the digitization reproduces exactly: Δ = (Asim − Amodel)/Asim, with σ the error of the mean (red bars; the blue bars are the halo scatter, ≈ 180 and ≈ 65 halos per bin).

Exponent convention. The printed fits write the outer factor as [1 + (x/xc)α]−β′. XGPaint's generalized_nfw uses the exponent −(β+γ)/α, so we pass βXG = αβ′ − γ, the same mapping XGPaint applies to Battaglia12. Against the paper's own drawn fit curves, this reading reproduces the shape to 0.3–3 % (max/min of model/paper over 0.04–1.34 R200c, with a constant 2–3 % offset); reading β′ as an asymptotic slope, −(β′ ∓ γ)/α, tilts the curves by 6–19 % (doc/check_beta_convention.py). An old XGPaint sign bug in the Battaglia12 exponent ((1+x)+β, commit b924ab1, March–April 2026) is in none of the maps used here.

The Battaglia curves drawn in Lee22 are not what HalfDome paints. Read from the vector figures, their Battaglia12 curve is the thermal pressure with the h-1M☉ mass used as M☉ (matches to 0.2 %), so it sits 1.93× above the electron pressure of the simulation curve. Their Battaglia16 curve is ρfit/(200 fb) with the exponent −(β−γ)/α instead of Battaglia's −(β+γ)/α, again with h-1M☉ as M☉ (matches to 0.1 %): fb is missing from ρgas (×6.4) and the outer slope is tilted. The Battaglia profiles as painted by XGPaint lie within 4–5 % of TNG at 0.3 < x < 1 in the lower bin and 24–28 % below it in the upper bin.

2. M1: why (1+z)3 and (1+z)2

The profiles were extracted with the public group_particles code (examples ne_prof.cpp, y_prof.cpp). TNG snapshots store lengths in comoving kpc/h. The shell volumes are built from the comoving Group_R_Crit200, but the normalisation uses the physical critical density at the snapshot time:

group_particles/examples/y_prof.cpp (Lee22 extraction)
// shells between logRmin..logRmax, from Group_R_Crit200 (ckpc/h)
return 4.0*M_PI/3.0*( std::exp(3.0*logR2) - std::exp(3.0*logR1) );
// per particle: electron pressure x particle volume
auto Y = 4.0*x*XH/(1.0+3.0*XH+4.0*XH*x) * (gamma-1.0) * m * e;
pressure[ii] /= shell_vol(ii);       // divided by a COMOVING volume

grp_P_t rho_crit () const {
    grp_P_t a3 = Time*Time*Time;
    return rho_crit_0*(Omega0/a3 + OmegaLambda); // PHYSICAL
}
// P200 with the comoving r = Group_R_Crit200
return 100.0*GNewton*m*rho_crit()*OmegaBaryon/Omega0/r;
group_particles/examples/ne_prof.cpp
auto ne = x * XH * m / mprot;       // electrons in the cell
e_density[idx] += ne;
...
e_density[ii] /= shell_vol(ii);      // COMOVING (ckpc/h)^3
// normalised later by n200 = 200 rho_crit(z) f_b /(X_H m_p),
// rho_crit(z) PHYSICAL (same helper as above)

Counting powers of a = 1/(1+z), with Vcom = Vphys/a3 and Rcom = Rphys/a:

nprinted / n200 = (N / Vcom) / n200 = a3 · ntrue/n200 density: only the volume carries a
Pprinted / P200,code = (a3 Ptrue) / (a P200) = a2 · Ptrue/P200 pressure: a3 from the volume, a−1 from the comoving R200 in the denominator of P200

So the true profiles are the printed ones times (1+z)3 and (1+z)2. This is an identity of the extraction code, valid at every redshift and equal to 1 at z = 0. Three independent checks:

  • Emulation. Applying the same bookkeeping to CAMELS-TNG gas cells gives emulated/true = a3 and a2 to better than 1 % at z = 0–2 (e.g. 0.1165 vs a3 = 0.1164 and 0.2380 vs a2 = 0.2384 at z = 1.05).
  • TNG model (CAMELS, 27 boxes). TNG / printed = 1.02 (1+z)1.84 ± 0.06 for the pressure and (1+z)3.1 for the density (1013.5–1014 M☉).
  • FLAMINGO (a different code and feedback model): the redshift slope of the printed fit is lower by 1.97–2.07 (pressure) and 2.9–3.1 (density) above 1013.6 M☉.

3. What is left after M1: Fn from the code units, FP = 1

After undoing M1, any constant offset between the fit and the TNG gas is a measured ratio. Both are defined the same way:

Fn = nprinted(1+z)3 / nsim,   FP = Pprinted(1+z)2 / Psim mean over 0.04–1.34 R200c; measured values in the table below

The measurements are FP ≈ 1 and Fn ≈ 5.5. Neither is used as a fitted constant: both follow from the units of Lee22's extraction code. The difference comes from where each profile is normalised.

group_particles/examples/ne_prof.cpp — density: counted in code units, normalised elsewhere

mprot = 0.6774*1.6726219/1.98847 * 1e-67;
auto ne = x * XH * m / mprot;        // electron COUNT (mass units cancel)
e_density[ii] /= shell_vol(ii);       // per (ckpc/h)^3: comoving AND h-scaled
// only e_density, M200 and R200 are written; n200 is applied later,
// outside this code (not public)
group_particles/examples/y_prof.cpp — pressure: normalised inside, same code units
// GNewton [kpc (km/s)^2 / 1e10 Msun], rho_crit_0 [1e10 Msun/h / (kpc/h)^3]
auto Y = ... * (gamma-1.0) * m * e;  // [1e10 Msun/h (km/s)^2]
pressure[ii] /= shell_vol(ii);        // / (ckpc/h)^3        → ∝ h^2
return 100.0*GNewton*m*rho_crit()*OmegaBaryon/Omega0/r;
                                      // P200 in the same units → ∝ h^2
density: electrons per (ckpc/h)3 read as per physical kpc3 → nstored = ntrue (a/h)3: the printed fit is low by a3 and high by 1/h3 Vphys = (a/h)3 Vcode: the a3 is M1, the 1/h3 = 3.22 is part of the same unit mistake
pressure: Pe and P200 both ∝ h2 in code units → h cancels; only a3/a = a2 survives this is why the pressure needs no constant (FP = 1) while the density does

The rest of Fn is the hydrogen convention. The paper counts electrons as Vine,i = ximiXH/mp (x = ElectronAbundance = ne/nH), whose natural normalisation is the hydrogen density nH,200 = 200 ρcrfbXH/mp. The paper prints n200 = 200 ρcrfb/(XHmp), which is neither the hydrogen nor the electron density. Reading the printed form literally overestimates ne by 1/XH2 = 1.73.

FP = 1 within 5–7 % in TNG, the simulation Lee22 fitted, exactly as the h-cancellation predicts; FLAMINGO's 20 % lower group pressure is a real difference between the simulations. Below 1013.5 M☉ both ratios drift because the fit is at its lower mass limit (1.5 × 1013 M☉). At z = 0, where M1 vanishes, Fig. 1 shows the factor directly: Lee22's own TNG density curve matches their fit, and both are 5.9× the TNG gas; the pressure curves agree. The constant is therefore in Lee22's conversion of the counts, not in the fitting.

The earlier empirical corrections

Two empirical corrections were used on the way to this result and are no longer final products. C(z) = 0.284 (1+z)2.48 was fitted to the TNG300 FRB halo-DM catalogue of Konietzka et al. ("1013–1014 M☉"), and Fn = 5.4 to the CAMELS gas. Fn = 5.4 agrees with the derived 5.57 to 3 %. C(z) does not, because the catalogue window most likely contains clusters: its "1013–1014" array behaves as if it held halos up to ~9 × 1014 M☉. The 1014–1015 window adds DM to only 0.07 / 0.23 / 0.26 % of rays at zs = 0.1 / 0.5 / 1, whereas 2.1 / 8.8 / 12.1 % of HalfDome rays cross such halos, and the windows are not disjoint. No boundary or electron convention (R200m, FoF cells, 57 ρcr cells, star-forming gas) reproduces both the extra electrons and the equal hit fractions; counting the clusters does, and then the catalogue needs Fn = 5.4 (1+z)−0.1, as the TNG gas does. The window reading is inferred from the arrays and should be confirmed with R. Konietzka.

const LEE2022_TNG_CALIBRATION_C0 = 0.2841790493880425      # C(z) fitted to Konietzka TNG300 halo DM
const LEE2022_TNG_CALIBRATION_K  = 2.483190184608004
const LEE2022_BOOKKEEPING_FN = parse(Float64, get(ENV, "LEE2022_BOOKKEEPING_FN", "5.4"))
const LEE2022_TNG_LITTLE_H = 0.6774                         # h hard-coded in Lee22's mprot

function lee2022_redshift_scaling_factor(model, z)
    model.redshift_scaling == :physical && return 1.0            # eq. 9 as printed
    model.redshift_scaling == :bookkeeping_physical &&                # final: derived, no fit
        return (LEE2022_TNG_LITTLE_H * (1 + z))^3 * model.hydrogen_mass_fraction^2
    model.redshift_scaling == :bookkeeping_a3 && return (1 + z)^3 / LEE2022_BOOKKEEPING_FN   # empirical check
    model.redshift_scaling == :tng_calibrated &&
        return LEE2022_TNG_CALIBRATION_C0 * (1 + min(z, 2.0))^LEE2022_TNG_CALIBRATION_K
    ...
end

XGPaint src/profiles_y.jl — the shared shape function (why βXG = αβ′ − γ)
function generalized_nfw(x, xc, α, β, γ)
    x̄ = x / xc
    return x̄^γ * (1 + x̄^α)^(-(β + γ) / α)   # with β = αβ′ − γ: exponent −β′, as printed
end

5. Results

FRB halo DM: HalfDome rays, analytic halo model and TNG

120,000 rays, gas inside each halo's R200c sphere. The analytic curves are the Torkamani et al. (2026) compound-Poisson halo model with clustering, evaluated with exactly the same densities. With the HalfDome halo abundance it reproduces the rays to 1–3 % for every model and source redshift, so rays and analytic model are interchangeable; in the TNG cosmology with a Tinker08 mass function down to 109 M☉ it is the analytic prediction for TNG.

Analytic reproduction of TNG (as in Torkamani+26 and Medlock+24)

What the papers compared. Torkamani et al. (2026) test only the total TNG300 DM distribution (IGM + halos, gas to 5 R200c, halos 108–1016 h-1M☉, mean taken from the Macquart relation), at z = 0.7–5. Medlock et al. (2024) use no halo model; they fit the Macquart form (F = 0.182) to CAMELS boxes. Neither tests halo-only DM or mass windows.

Their test reproduces. Our engine with Torkamani's BFC parameters matches the TNG total-DM distribution to KS distance 0.03–0.05 (zs = 0.3–1), 10–14 % narrower; the Macquart form with F = 0.182 does not (KS ≈ 0.10; TNG300's own F is 0.245). This test says little about halo interiors: BFC puts 83 % of its halo DM outside R200c.

Halo-only. With the "1013–1014" array read as [1013, 9 × 1014] M☉ and TNG300's hydro halo abundance (0.81 of Tinker08 there, measured from the ray hit fractions), the derived Lee22 density gives 0.96 / 0.97 / 0.97 of the TNG mean at zs = 0.1 / 0.5 / 1, KS 0.02–0.025, flat in zs; Battaglia16 and BFC give ≈ 0.8 and the previous ×0.6016 falls from 2.8 to 1.4. For the window-free "all halos" array every smooth profile fails (derived Lee22 0.70 / 0.63 / 0.55): 58 % of TNG's DM at zs = 1 comes from halos below 1013 M☉, where TNG holds 0.3–0.5 fb of electrons inside R200c and the extrapolated Lee22 fit 0.01–0.2.

Not the finite box. TNG300 (205 Mpc/h) holds 278 / 152 / 49 halos of 1014–1015 M☉ at z = 0 / 0.5 / 1. The ray hit fraction depends on their number density, not on the box volume, so a lightcone built from these snapshots should see such a halo on 2.0 / 8.3 / 11.6 % of rays (lowest of 20,000 simulated boxes 0.95 / 3.6 / 5.2 %). The catalogue shows 0.065 / 0.23 / 0.26 %, 30–40× fewer; the ×9 window reading predicts 0.066 / 0.20 / 0.22 %.

tSZ: y power and tSZ × FRB DM

Five NSIDE 4096 y maps were painted in one catalogue pass on idark: Battaglia12 and Lee22 as printed (both with exact per-halo profiles below z = 0.1), Lee22 × (1+z)2, the hybrid (Battaglia12 above 1014.8 h-1M☉) and the calibrated-range map. Every map matches the exact per-halo sum over its own catalogue to 1–4 % at ℓ = 100–3000.
